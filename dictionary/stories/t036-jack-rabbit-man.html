Jack Rabbit man

36 lines · from Melville Jacobs’s Coos texts, dictated by Annie Miner Peterson (1933–34); keyed 1986–1990.

1

łáəx̣wə́n dɛ́·məł

jack rabbit man

2

ə́·· háma-wántł-yuxʷúm-ɛ.

e:<e I suppose I will be traveling around now.

3

wɛ́n-íldᵘwa-tłə-dəhú·mis

That is what he said to his wife.

4

g̣ɛ́ndji-hántł-g̯ɛ-ɛn ɛ'yúxʷúmɛ́

Where are you going to be journeying?

5

án-úk̓wá·niyadɛ-g̣ɛ́ndjuwi.

I do not know where to.

6

yuwút's-hantł-qʷcídjɛ-ku-uł·á.

Maybe I will be going to the south.

7

tsú-wɛn-í·lt.

That is what he said.

8

tsú-łá.

Then he went.

9

tsú-balditcá-dji,

Now he got to the ocean beach,

10

tsu-də-wɛ́·n-há·t̓i.

and then he sang in this manner.

11

gwɛlɛ́·niyɛ gwɛlɛ́·niyɛ

gwelee/niye gwelee/niye

12

gwəlíswa gwəlíswa

North wind blow north wind blow

13

tsú-du-ditc-k̯łá·wi tsú-du-dataltál·i,

And when he saw something he would dance and sing,

14

hahá'əndá'nəs hahá'əndá'nəs

To the goal to the goal

15

tsú-qʷcídjɛ́ʻ-dji,

Now he got to the south,

16

tsú-g̣ɛk̯ɛ'lɛ́yɛ,

and he began to halloo,

17

hɛ́·dɛ-dihí-mɛ

Rich person's children

18

g̣ɛ́lidɛ́itcil

you ferry me across

19

tsú-du-gɛ́ʻ-k̓áʻ-g̣ál·ats,

So a person went (across) there to get him,

20

an-dú-idjáu-k̓áʻ'idjɛ.

but found no one anywhere.

21

hɛi-dú-his-há wɛ́-s-ditc-tłə-k̓áʻ,

And when that person had just gotten back again,

22

hɛi-dú-má·tsi dá·s-k̓áʻ-k̯'ál·a

then again that person (jack rabbit) hallooed.

23

tsú-du-dá·s g̣ál·ats tłə-k̓áʻ,

And so again that person went (across) to get him,

24

an-dú-idjáutc-ídjɛ

but he saw nothing anywhere.

25

tsú-bɛ́lx̣səm,

Now he was angry,

26

tsú-hɛ́ʻgwən,

27

28

29

hɛ́i-tła-łałx̣wə́n daʻ-dlú·g̣wa,

and only a jack rabbit sat there,

30

tsú-kwi·-tsá·u.

31

tsú-wə́s·i.

Then he went back.

32

33

ú· tsi-da-x̯-kúwi kwə-łáłx̣wən,

uu/u I suppose it must have been just that jack rabbit,

34

tsí-dɛ-x̯-hídji á-ak̯'álai.

it must have been just he who was hallooing.

35

ánya-wi-k̯'ál·a ya-u-kwi·-qwə́t'cda.

36

tsí-kú·wi ku-lag̣auyát̓as.
