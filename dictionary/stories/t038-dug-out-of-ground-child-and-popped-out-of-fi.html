Dug-out-of-ground child and popped out of fire

76 lines · from Melville Jacobs’s Coos texts, dictated by Annie Miner Peterson (1933–34); keyed 1986–1990.

1

ú·btíyat̓á·məs k̯̓íl·ga,

Dug-out-of-ground child,

2

tławux̣tíyat̓á·məs'itc

(and) popped-out-of-fire

3

gwɛ́is-da-dlú·g̣wa

A girl lived there (with her brother and grandfather).

4

wí-mí·t'ci du·-kwí·-tsí·m-tłə-gwɛ́is,

Though the girl slept alone,

5

hɛi-du ́-dɛ́n-gɛ-qłəm

every night some person came to sleep with her.

6

ma·-dú-hidjúwi-k̓áʻ-tsú·m.

The person who came to sleep with her never spoke.

7

an-dú-íl·at tłə-k̓áʻ-hidjúwi-tsú·m.

He came to her there a long time.

8

hɛ́··niyɛ g̣ɛ́ʻ-kwi·-tdjíyim.

And then the girl became pregnant.

9

hɛi-má·tsi múwɛłdiyɛ tłə-gwɛ́is·án-dji-kwi·-k̓wa·naná'ya,

She did not say anything of it (the never speaking person with her),

10

ɛ́lqs

she was afraid to.

11

tsú-wɛ·n-dɛ'lúwɛ.

Now this is what she thought.

12

páix̯ts-kwə-'ni̯k̓íłan.

I will paint my hand,

13

x̣-wɛ́n-wantł̯-k̓íł·dwa i-da-wí.

That is the way I will find out who it may be.

14

á·yu-mɛ-x̣-wɛ́·n.

Indeed that is the way (she did).

15

tsú-x'áł-i'ya,

And then (in the night) she hugged him,

16

ts'á·yu dət'łá·-ya di̯k̓í·łan.

she put her hand on his back.

17

tsú-hantł-x̣ʷdlá·t's tłə-k̓áʻ-x̣ux̣ú·djə dlí·gwɛkʷ

Now people were going to exit the sweat house they slept in.

18

tsú-sədłə́ntc łú·dədaya,

And so she watched in secret,

19

tsú-gu·s-sə́lt̓-də-k̓aʻ.

and then all those people (men) came out.

20

hɛi-má·tsi-mí·dəsáma-dəhɛ́t'łɛ kwí·-xkwí'ya tłə-kwí-an-x̣-wɛ́n-wá·tsa

Indeed now (she learned) it was her own older brother who was doing her ill like that.

21

wí-má·tsi-x̣ɛ́nx̣ɛnu tłə-gwɛ́is,

Then the girl became sick,

22

hís-hidjí-q̓áyau,

and he also died,

23

t'łáɣ·iya-də́t̓ɛ,

he starved himself (in his shame and grief),

24

x̣-wɛ́n-his-hídji-q̓áyau.

and that is why he died too.

25

wí·-yə-q̓áyau wi·-hɛ́məlt̓dɛ-djɛ́ʻ-tsú·wi'ya tłə-dɛx̣-ɛ́ł·ɛ.

When she died her father (her father) laid her upon the fire.

26

wí-naqt tłə-dək̓áʻ dət'łhái,

And he held an Indian blanket,

27

wí-wɛ́n-íl·at tłə-tú·'mət'ł,

and the old man spoke thus,

28

tłɛ́wux̣tɛ'mí·yɛx̣

Pop out on to this here

29

á·yu-g̣ɛ́-tłáutim-tłə̯-k̓íl·ga

Sure enough the baby popped out.

30

wí-kwi-há·wi-ya-tłə-dɛx̣-tsqwə́lq̓wtc

Now its grandfather brought it up.

31

wí-má·-g̣ɛnɛ́·tc-k̓aʻ gu·s-mín-du·-kwi·-g̣ɛ́m yug̣wa'áma

other young persons were always digging camas.

32

wí-wá·-g̣ɛm du·-lɛ́·mɛm.

There were large camas stuck down (growing) there.

33

tsú-tłə-gwɛ́is,

Now the (youngest) girl (said),

34

kwí·-wántł-wáupts.

I will dig that.

35

ku·-wá·-g̣ɛm wí·-yu-wáupts,

Then when she dug up a large camas,

36

hɛi-má·tsi̯-k̓íl·ga ú·biya'itc,

she dug out a baby indeed,

37

di·'lút'ł.

a boy.

38

tsú-kwi-wɛ́n-íl·at tłə-dahɛnɛ́·k̓wn,

And then her older sister spoke thus,

39

tbí·ts

Bury it

40

an-údu·há'ya

I do not want it.

41

ha·-dú-ditc ɣəlɣəlyə́q̓ham.

They only just gossip and say things

42

án-t'swa'-al

Do not bother with it

43

an-g̯ɛ́·.

Why no.

44

wus·á·ya-wantł,

I will take it back,

45

há·wiya-wantł.

I will raise it.

46

hɛ́lt̓-ha-dzə'nɛ́·wɛt'ł.

It is just so very cute.

47

á·yu-wus·á·ya,

So indeed she took it home,

48

tsú-há·wiya-tłə-x̣-gwɛ́is

and that girl brought it up.

49

wí·-kwí-ya-itc'yɛ́ik̯it'cínu'wiyɛ tłətc-hí·mɛ

Now those boys began to be chums,

50

gu·s-mín-itc-du·-q̓ɛ́icəcɛ́·nu.

they were always playing the strike-the-ball game.

51

wi·-tłətc-tu·'mə́t'ł dədím·ísin,

The grandson of the old man,

52

mái-du·q̓ú·s du·-k̓wɛ́·səs ma·-dú-tsx̣a,

no matter how hard the ball he would split it anyhow,

53

gu·s-mín-kwi·-k̓wɛ́·səs-dzí·ya tłə-dɛx̣-tsqwə́l-q̓wtc

every ball that his grandfather made.

54

ha·'wíyam-tłə-hí·mɛ.

The children grew up.

55

há· gu·s-mín-u·-k̓wɛ́·səs-dzí·ya

Haa/. I will make balls all the time.

56

kwə́-'nəq̓ú·łɛx̣ɛ́-x̣ɛs.

Now my own ankle bone (I will make a ball of).

57

á·yu-łú·-dá-hálkʷt,

Indeed he took it out,

58

kwíyɛ-dək̓wɛ́·səs tłə-dədím·sin.

and then his grandson had it for a ball.

59

mín·t'ci tsə-q̓láɣdzəmau.

Once (one day) he hit the other (boy).

60

ə́·-· tłáwəx̣tiyat̓á-məs

@@/@@ popped-out-of-fire

61

q̓ál-autáinɛ.

you hurt me.

62

wí-his-nənɛ́u-u·btíyat̓á·məs.

Oh you yourself you (are named) dug-out-of-ground.

63

tsú-itc-an-dá·s-alicá·nu

Then they did not play (together) any more.

64

ú·-anántł-balax̣á·nidai.

Oh do not be angry with me.

65

á·· in-án-du·há'ya balax̣á·nida·mi,

aa/a if you do not want me to be angry at you,

66

nɛ́u-nantł-bíldiuwi-ła,

you will go north,

67

wí-ɛ́n·ɛ-qʷcidjú·wi-wan-lá.

68

wi-ís-hantł-hí·dimɛu wí-yɛik̯it'cín·u'wiyɛ s-hántł-dá·s-ás·u.

If we should meet then we will be friends again.

69

áyu-itc-mɛ́-x̣-wɛ́-n.

70

71

ma·-dú-wɛ·n-tsʻál·du,

72

wí-tsu-du-t̓ə́ma-dáʻ-ła

73

məsá-itc-wɛ́·n-ła mí·gək̓á·ní·da

74

hɛi-ítc-yu-hí·dzəmɛu,

75
