At death the heart went

19 lines · from Melville Jacobs’s Coos texts, dictated by Annie Miner Peterson (1933–34); keyed 1986–1990.

1

i-du-k̓áʻ-q̓áyau wí-íbitbíu,

When a person died and he was buried,

2

wí-wɛ·n-du-ɣá·lá·nu tłə́-k̓a.

this is what the people said.

3

án-dú·-dzɛ́·t'łə́sdə,

It is not his skin,

4

an-dú·-lá·'mák̯də.

not his bones (that live on).

5

ma·-dú-dáʻ-dlu·g̣wa tlə-dəlá·'mak̯.

His bones indeed stay right here.

6

wí-tłə-də'lúwɛ,

But his heart,

7

wí-g̣ɛnt'cínsi-g̣ahá·'ya,

five days afterwards,

8

wí·-t̓ə́ma há·g̣wiya tłɛ-x̣-lúwɛ tłə-də-dzɛ́·t'łis.

that is the time that his heart leaves his skin (body).

9

wí·-g̣wá·n-wə́s·í-tłə-dəma ·́ni'ya·sdja.

Then it (he) goes back up to his parents.

10

x̣-wɛ́·n-tłɛ-x̣-k̓áʻ kwi·-tłq̓á·-ya tłə-k̓áʻ-q̓yaᵘ.

That is what the people believed about a ka when he died.

11

áɴ-huwɛ tłə-du·-kwi·-gú·s djict'cí·lu tłə-dətɛ́·tc,

Then they burned all his garments,

12

náim-hidjím·ił-tɛ́·tc.

because they were his own garments.

13

náim x̣-wɛ́·n-íldⁿwa tłɛ́-x̣-k̓sʻ-dəmá·ni'ya·s.

Because that was what the people's father said.

14

án·ántł-du dítc-náqt.

You are not to retain anything (of his).

15

yú-wút's-nɛ x̣-g̣ɛ́t x̣ɛ́nwɛ' ɛs-g̣álám.

From it you might catch a sickness.

16

wɛ́·n-ditc tłɛ-x̣-k̓áʻ tłə-du·-kwí· gú·s t'cá·l tłɛ-x̣-k̓áʻ tłə-dətɛ́·tc,

That is why the people burned everything of a person's garments,

17

tłə-k̓áʻ-q̓ayaⁿ.

of a person who died.

18

náim-wɛ́n-íldⁿwa tłɛ-x̣-k̓áʻ-dəmá·-ni'ya·s.

Because that is what the people's father told them.

19

wɛ́n-ditc-du·-tłɛ́-x̣-k̓áʻ tłə-kwí·-gu·s t'cá·l tłə-dəgú·s-didjɛ́·nɛn-ditc tłə-k̓áʻ-q̓ayaᵘ.

That is why the people burned everything of the belongings of a person who had died.

← A shaman who lost control of his pain-power Seeing a person's spirit double →

The voice in these texts is Annie Miner Peterson’s, recorded by Melville Jacobs in 1933–34.
