The girls who wished to have stars for their husbands

35 lines · from Melville Jacobs’s Coos texts, dictated by Annie Miner Peterson (1933–34); keyed 1986–1990.

1

yú·mi dúha'ya hí·mɛ kwi-ídə-iłdɛdɛ́·mił

The girls who wished to have stars for their husbands

2

g̣ɛ́nɛ·tc-k̓áʻ gú·s-mín-ił-du-gu·s-djí'-x̣áłiłi

Young persons were always doing something or other.

3

tsú-gwum-qánu·dja gum-dlí·kʷtim.

Now they were going to lie down to sleep outside.

4

tsú-gwum-g̣wá·n-hamg̣aida,

They looked up above,

5

gúm-wɛ́-n-ɣa·lá·'nu,

and they said thus,

6

yú·mi-ł-hantł-dɛ́·mił

Let us have stars for husbands!

7

dzáwá-tłə-g̣ɛ́nɛ·tc-k̓a

There were four girls (there).

8

g̣atq̓ái-yú·mi kwi-'nántł-dɛ́·mił.

I will have the evening star for my husband.

9

wí-ɛ́n·ɛ wí·-kwɛx̣tc-k̓áʻ wɛ́·n-lat'cíya·da yú·mi łímda·was,

I will have the star (that one) that the people call the hunters,

10

łímdí·da yú·-mi.

the hunter star.

11

kuwí-'nantl-dɛ́·mił.

That is what I will have for a husband.

12

wí-ɛ́n·ɛ kwətc-ha-łə́n·wi dləhaháyam,

And I the one that shines so brightly,

13

kúwi-'nantł-dɛ́·mił.

that one I will have for my husband.

14

wí-ɛ́n·ɛ kwətc-ha̯-ɛ́·k̓,

And I that very little one,

15

áɴ-djiʻ łə́n·wi-dləhaháyam

it barely shines at all.

16

ɛ́·k̯̓ k̓á·-dax̯,

It must be a small person,

17

náu-kwi̯-ɛ́·k̓ yú·mi.

which is why it is a small star.

18

kwíya-ł-hantł-gɛ́·qł

Oh let us go to sleep

19

tsú-du·-t'łɛ́wə-x̯ ɛx̯ ɛ́idu tłɛ-gɛ́nɛ·tc-k̓áʻ

And the girls laughed and laughed then.

20

wí-ił-kwi·-dlán̯k̓ts,

Now they woke up,

21

hɛⁱ-má·tsi-á·yú-itc gú·s-ił-nðdɛ́·mə́łɛ.

and to be sure they each had a man.

22

tłətc̯-ɛ́·k̓ dɛ́·mił dú·ha'ya,

The one who wanted a small husband,

23

hɛⁱ-má·tsi tú·'mít'ł-ditc-itc,

now she had only an extremely old man,

24

tłə-dəsɛ́ʟ tłə-dəhá·mis má·tsi gwa-kʷdlí·s tłə-dəx̣-yá·láq

his head (and) hair was grey hair just like foam ( of the ocean).

25

wí-tłətc łə́n·wi dlahaháyam dú·ha'ya,

And the one who wanted the brightly shining (star),

26

wí-nəhɛ́·wudzən dí·lúł.

she had a handsome young man.

27

wí-tłətc-q̓awá'mis dú·ha'ya,

The one who wanted the evening star,

28

wí-nəhɛ́·wudzən wá·-dɛ́·mił.

she had a big handsome man.

29

wí-tłə-dítc má·ʻ-dú·ha'ya wí-nəg̣ug̣wí·lɛ tłə-dədɛ́·mił,

The other one who wanted the one with a bow (weapon) for her husband,

30

his-kwí-nə-hɛ́·wudzən.

he was handsome too.

31

hɛ́n·ɛ́łdu kwí·-ł-du-gubə́bə í·ł-du łímda·wa.

We are lined up when we hunt.

32

x̣-wɛ́n íl-dᵘwa tłə-gwɛ́is

That is what he said to the girl.

33

x̣-wɛ́·n-ditc tłə-du-x̣-k̓áʻ kwi·-wɛ́·n sə́n·á'na,

That is why the people name them that way,

34

kwə-du·tc-gwu-bə́ba yú·mi

those lined up stars.

35

tsú-tsi-wɛ́·s

Now it is so much of that.

The voice in these texts is Annie Miner Peterson’s, recorded by Melville Jacobs in 1933–34.
