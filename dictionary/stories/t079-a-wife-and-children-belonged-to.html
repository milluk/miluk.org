A wife and children belonged to

24 lines · from Melville Jacobs’s Coos texts, dictated by Annie Miner Peterson (1933–34); keyed 1986–1990.

1

i-du-tłu·tłú·yu tłə-gwɛ'is,

When a girl had been bought (in marriage),

2

wí-i-du-g̣ɛ́ʻ-dlúqʷsiᴍ,

and when she lived there (with the husband),

3

ányá-du tłə-dəmá·ni'ya·s-dəditc.

she was no longer her parents' thing.

4

tłə-dədɛ́·mił-dəmá·ni'yas kwí·yɛ́·-du hɛn̯í·k̓is hidjím·ił-má·ni'ya·s,

Her husband's parents were like her own (parents) parents henceforth,

5

djí·-x-dúwa tłə-dəx̣-mí·disa·ma.

just like her own people.

6

wɛ́·n-ił-du-wá·wa tłi-iłdimidú·n,

That was the way they were to their daughter-in-law,

7

náu-du ányá kwíyɛ́·-dəditc tłə-dəmá·ni'ya·s.

because she was no longer her parents' thing.

8

i-nəhí·mɛ́·dí·yiq,

When she had children,

9

wí-tłə-də' ɛłɛ́' ɛdjú·-wi g̣ɛ́ʻ-iłt'łɛ́·yim,

they adhered to their father's (side) there,

10

tłi-iłdə' ɛłɛ́·-d(th)ə́k̓aʻ,

to their father's people,

11

áɴ-ɛ́ndjintc'iłdə-t'łɛ́·yim.

they did not adhere to their mother.

12

ɛłɛ́·djintc-'iłdə g̣adíya-k̓a-ił,

They belonged to their father's people,

13

his-tłə-íłdə'ɛ́nɛ wí-his-kúwi g̣adíya-k̓a'áya,

and their mother also belonged to those people,

14

náyim-g̣ɛʻ-dɛ́młtsim

because she had married there.

15

x̣-wɛ́n tłɛ-k̓áʻ tłi-iłd(th)ətá·má·łis,

That was the custom of the people,

16

kwí-iłtłq̓á·ya,

which they followed.

17

náu-du-mái q̓áyau-dədɛ́·mił,

Even if the husband died,

18

máʻ-daʻ-dlú·g̣wa,

they stayed there anyway,

19

náim-áɴ-dji tłə-dəmá·ní·cdja bi yat̓a tłə-dəhí·mɛ,

because she could not take back her children to her parents,

20

náim-tłi-iłdɛ' ɛ́łɛ g̣adíya'ił tłə-kúwi-t'łdá·yá·s.

for they belonged to their father's place.

21

wɛ́·n-ditc tłi-ił-du áɴ-ɛ́n·ɛ-dít'łdácdja aɴ-dú-g̣ɛ́'-wusəsíyam,

That is why they never went back to their ( mom's ) mother's place,

22

náim-áɴ-da·s-g̣adíya-k̓áʻ,

because they did not belong to those people any more,

23

i-tlú·-tlúyu tłi-iłdɛ'ɛ́nɛ.

when their mother had been purchased (in marriage).

24

wɛ́·n-tłə-k̓aʻ-dətá·má·łis.

That was the people's custom.

The voice in these texts is Annie Miner Peterson’s, recorded by Melville Jacobs in 1933–34.
