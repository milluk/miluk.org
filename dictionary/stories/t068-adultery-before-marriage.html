Adultery before marriage

49 lines · from Melville Jacobs’s Coos texts, dictated by Annie Miner Peterson (1933–34); keyed 1986–1990.

1

tłɛ-hɛ́·niyɛ́-k̓áʻ-dətá·má·łis,

Long ago (this was) the custom of the people,

2

tli-iłhidjím·ił-didjá·ní'was.

their way of doing.

3

wí-yu-wɛ́n-djín-djinyɛq̓hɛm tli-hí·mɛ húmɛ̯·k̓ɛ,

If they thought a young woman,

4

yuwút's hí·mɛ dɛ́·mił wí-ił x̣-wɛ́n-djindjina djí-aɴ-hu·dzi·da,

perhaps a young man whom they thought was doing something wrong

5

wí-kwi·-du tłə-k̓áʻ-nəgwáicdja wí-mitcmín·át'c tłə-dəgwá·ya.

then the person whose daughter it was would ask his (daughter) daughter.

6

wí-ya-áɴ-tłi,

If she said nothing,

7

wí-gígwáʻ-k̯tá-hɛ́ludə.

he would cut (with a knife) just a little on her face.

8

wí-wɛ́·n-tłi,

Then she would say,

9

má-wu-án-x̣-wí dji-x̣ałdú·n.

No one has done anything to me.

10

án-hɛwɛ·sɛnu'wiyix̓

Do not lie

11

tsú'wá'mí·nax̣.

I might kill you (if you lie).

12

wí-ya-áyu-áɴ-dji-x̣áłtim,

If indeed she had done nothing,

13

wí-má-x̣-wɛ·n-íl·at.

she would continue to speak thus.

14

wí-tłə-ɛ́stísdə tłi-hí·mɛ-húm̯ɛ́·k̓ɛ,

But other young women,

15

wí-yá-áɴ-tli,

if they said nothing,

16

má'yuk-gú·s-idjáu-iłk̯tá,

even though cut all over,

17

wí-ma-áɴ-tłi.

they would continue to say nothing.

18

wí-k̓wá·ni'yada'ił náim-áɴ-tłi.

Then they would know because she had not spoken.

19

x̣-wɛ·'n-ił-du-wá·tsa tłi-hí·mɛ́·-k̓aʻ.

That is how they would do to young people.

20

wí-i-x̣-wɛ́·n-tłí,

If she spoke thus,

21

x̣-á·yu-w̯í-k̓x̣íwun'yɛis.

He was only talking to me.

22

tsi-u-g̯ɛ-áɴ-t'swu'lí·n,

He did not bother me,

23

wí-tsu-du-t̓ə́ma g̣ɛ́ʻ-k̓aʻ-wúlx̣íyu tłə-dí·lul-də'yɛ́·dzədjɛ,

then he would send a person (person) to the house of the youth,

24

wí-g̣wəsg̣ú·yu tłə-də"má·ni'yás.

and tell his parents.

25

wí-yu-wɛ́·n-tłi,

Then if he (the father of the youth) would speak thus,

26

mant'ct-wántl kwə-'ni̯k̓íl-ga,

I will ask my child,

27

wí-á-yu-ditdjíyu tłə-dí·lul.

and they would fetch in the youth.

28

wí-í-wá·'nu,

If he said so,

29

wí-wɛ́·n-tłə-də'ɛ́łɛ,

and his father (said) thus,

30

sg̣u·-yá-nantł kwə-k̓áʻ nəgwáicdja,

Tell the person whose daughter it is,

31

wí-dú·ha'yani'í ɛhú·mis

(the messenger would ask) Do you want the woman

32

wí-yu-wá·'nu,

If he said,

33

dú·ha'ya'wi,

I want her,

34

wí-tłá·wi.

he bought her (in marriage).

35

wí-ya-áɴ-x̣-wɛ·n'nəmɛ,

But if that was not done,

36

ya-ámi-hadái'mis,

if moneyless,

37

án-x̣-dji tłɛ́-hadái'mis g̣álam,

if they could not get any money,

38

wí-gɛʻ-q̓x̣ɛ́yu tłə-dək̓ʷhána·s tłə-dəłə́n·ɛx̣.

they would cut off his ears and his nose.

39

wí-yuwút's at̓á·dədɛ́·djɛ má-wucdjɛ́u halqáimad(th)ə wí-x̣-kwí-sg̯itúwat̓a.

Sometimes his paternal (father's) aunt or any relative would pay for his life.

40

x̣-wɛ́·n tlɛ-k̓áʻ tłə-kwí-án-x̣-wɛ·n-ditc-dzí·ya,

That is why the people did not do things like that,

41

náim wá·g̣a tłi-iłdidjá·ni'wa·s.

because it was very big (serious) to do that.

42

wɛ́·n-ditc tłɛ-x̣-tcánɣa tłə-kwi áɴ-idjáu mí·t'ci ɣá·lá·nu tłi-hí·mɛ́·-k̓aʻ.

That is why young people did not talk together alone ( together by themselves).

43

yu-wút's-itc-mís·a yɛ-hɛthɛ́·dɛ-k̓áʻ-dihí·mɛ,

Sometimes if both were children of very-well-to-do people,

44

wí-ił-g̣ɛ́ʻ-ła'yúwa tłɛ-x̣-hú·mis-d(th)ə-'ɛ́łɛ,

and the father of the girl would go to there,

45

wí-ámi-iłhadái'mis,

and they had no money,

46

wí-i-tsú·tsu tłə-di̯k̓ílga.

he would kill the youth.

47

wí-hɛlt̓ tłɛ-x̣-dí·luł-də-'ɛ́łɛ x̣-kwí-hɛlt̓-tsá·u tłə-gwɛ́is.

Then the father of the youth would kill the girl (the girl) also.

48

wɛ́n-iłtsá·waⁿ tłɛ́-x̣-k̓áʻ.

That is what the people did.

49

wɛ́·n-ditc tłi-il-kwi-álqsa.

That is why they were afraid of that.
