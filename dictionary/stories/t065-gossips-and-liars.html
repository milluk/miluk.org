Gossips and liars

20 lines · from Melville Jacobs’s Coos texts, dictated by Annie Miner Peterson (1933–34); keyed 1986–1990.

1

tłɛ-hɛ́·niyɛ-k̓áʻ,

(Among) the people of long ago,

2

i-du-k̓áʻ gu·s-míɴ-k̓áʻ-íldᵘwa,

when a person was incessantly talking about people,

3

gu·s-míɴ-hɛ́wɛ·sɛ́·nu,

(and) always telling lies (about them),

4

wí-kwi-yímł-qdlaⁱ i-du-áɴ-sk̓at tłə-dɛx̣-hɛ́wɛs,

if he did not pay for such a lie,

5

wí-kwi-yímł-qdlaⁱ du-dliłdjí·lu.

then they would have a fight because of it.

6

x-wɛ́·n tłɛ́-k̓aʻ tłə-dətá·má·łis.

That was the custom of the people.

7

wi-i-hí·mɛ du-k̓wɛ́·n-dá-gdlaⁱ,

If a child was (also) telling (lies) about such a matter,

8

kwi'yɛ́·nu-k̓á-wulmɛᵘ.

the people would fight about that.

9

wí-g̣ɛ́-du-tittíu tłi̯-k̓ílga.

And they would throw that child right into there

10

wí-á·yú-du ánya-kwa'anyá·t'ł.

Then it would tell tales no longer.

11

x̯-wɛ́·n il-du-wá·tsan tłi-hí·mɛ yu-kwa'ányát'ł.

That is what they did to a child when it gossiped.

12

wá·g̣a-ditc tłɛ́-x̣-k̓áʻ i-iłhɛ́-wɛ·su·tc-dítc k̓wa·naná'ya'ił

It was a great thing (a serious offence) when a person told lies.

13

wɛ́n-ditc tli-ił-áɴ-k̓aʻ-hawa·sána'ya,

That is why people did not lie,

14

ná·yim yuwút's wá·g̣a-ditc,

because it might be a big (serious) thing,

15

kwi'yɛ́·nu yuwút's tsú·tsu tłɛ-k̓áʻ-gú·s-míɴ k̓áʻ-íldᵘwa

and for that they might kill persons who spoke (lies) about people all the time.

16

x̣-wɛ́·n-ditc tłɛ-x̣-k̓áʻ tłi-ił-áɴ-wɛ·n wá·tsa,

That is why people did not do that,

17

náim-ił-áɴ-dú·ha'ya tłɛ-hɛ́us-dú-k̓aʻ,

because they did not like a liar,

18

náim·áɴ-wɛ-n-didjá·ni'wa·s,

because that was not a good thing,

19

wá·g̣a-ditc.

(it was) a very big thing.

20

x̣-wɛ́n-ditc tłɛ-x̣-k̓áʻ tłi-ił-áɴ-x̣-wɛ́·n-ditc tsá·waᵘ

That is why the people did not have (do) that sort of thing.
