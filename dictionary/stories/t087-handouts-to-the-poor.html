Handouts to the poor

10 lines · from Melville Jacobs’s Coos texts, dictated by Annie Miner Peterson (1933–34); keyed 1986–1990.

1

tłə-kwí·'nɛ'wɛt'ł-k̓áʻ i-du-ámi-q̓wə́n·yau,

Whenever a poor person lacked food,

2

wi·-łú·dədáya-du idjá·u g̣wə́-l·ɛ'ɛs-k̯łá·wi,

he watched for where he saw smoke,

3

g̣ɛ́ʻ-du·-dɛ́·djɛ.

and into there he went.

4

á·yu-du q̓atskí·nu.

He was given food indeed.

5

wí-i-du-ɛ́·wi didlu'wá·was wí-wa·'nú·-du,

When he had finished eating he would say,

6

di-nántł-ti·nt'c.

I'll take a gift of food.

7

wí-á·yu-du-q̓atskí·nu,

Then indeed he would be given food,

8

gá·ł-du-ditc-ni'ní·yu.

he would be given a lot.

9

wí-kwi·-du-wəsí·ya·t̓a tłə-dəyɛ́·tsədjə,

He would take them back with him to his own home,

10

hit'cuwá·-du gú·s tłətc-gwí·du.

he would gather up all the leftovers.

← Poor people who became wealthy The chiefs kept the poor from going hungry →

The voice in these texts is Annie Miner Peterson’s, recorded by Melville Jacobs in 1933–34.
