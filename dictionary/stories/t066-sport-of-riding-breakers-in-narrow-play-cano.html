Sport of riding breakers in narrow play canoes

8 lines · from Melville Jacobs’s Coos texts, dictated by Annie Miner Peterson (1933–34); keyed 1986–1990.

1

tlə-báldiyá·-k̓áʻ x-wɛ́·n-iłdə'álicá·ní'wa·s tłi-iłdətłgwə́ls

The oceanside ka had sport they played in this manner with their canoes.

Linked dictionary entry (1)

k̯̓a' people, person

2

dzú·nugwa-səbɛ́·b.

They were double enders.

Linked dictionary entries (2)

dzunug side · gwubaba line up

3

wɛ́·n-ił-du-alicá·nída.

This is how they played with them.

4

báldi·məs dag̣ála'yu ił-du-tłhínpiyam

They would go right through inside the surf.

Linked dictionary entries (2)

daga upriver · tlhinpya gone through, be able to

5

wí·-ił-du·-tsʻál·du wí-məsá·-du-wɛ́·n-tsx̣á·t's,

When they had gone past it they both stepped that way,

Linked dictionary entries (3)

misa both · ts'ál·du to get through · tsx̣a split, stepped on

6

á·yu-du-gú·s-x̣t̓ú-tlə-há·p̓.

and then all the water was dipped out (of the boat).

Linked dictionary entries (3)

gú··s all, always · há·pʼ water · x̣t̓ú dipper out ?

7

wɛ́n-ił-du-álicá·ní·da.

That was their sport.

8

an-íł-du-hít'ci-tłbíliya,

No one (not many of them) upset,

Linked dictionary entries (3)

hí·t'ci one, half · íł they · tlbilya tip over
