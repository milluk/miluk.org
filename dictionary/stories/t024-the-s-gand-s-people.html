The sɛ́gandá·s people

98 lines · from Melville Jacobs’s Coos texts, dictated by Annie Miner Peterson (1933–34); keyed 1986–1990.

1

tłə-sə́gandá·s-k̓áʻ

The se<ganda:<s people

2

dá-hɛthɛ́·dɛ dłú·g̣wa tłə-tcɛ́·t̓i,

That (giant) wealthy head man lived at Chetco (River),

3

wí·-dáʻ-di·g̣ɛdiu kwí-mit'łda·'yá·s.

and their village was on this side (to the north) of it.

4

wí-gɛ́'-gadíya-də'ɛ́nɛ tłítc-gwa-dí·łúł tłə́-Crəscənt City>'ídjɛ.

The mother of that (se<ganda:<s) boy came from ( Crescent ) Crescent City.

5

tsú-sbái-də'wá·ł-'wał tłɛx̣s-gwa-dí·łúł.

The young fellow was always sharpening his knife.

6

djíʻ-g̯ɛ́-n̥sbá·i-nə'wá·ł'wał

Why do you sharpen your knife?

7

ə́· g̣ɛ́ʻ-wantł-łáqʷcídjɛ k̯łá·wi-nantł nə'ax̯íyaxitc.

Oh I am going to go to the south to see my mother's brother.

8

áɴ-dji·-ditc íł·dunwɛ

Do not say so

9

wudłám·aq-k̓áʻ wí-an-dú-dji dáʻ-kwi·-tsʻáł·du.

There are giant people who do not let you pass by.

10

áɴ-wɛ́·n-k̓aʻ kwi-iłdɛhɛthɛ́·dɛ.

Their wealthy head man is a person of mean spirit.

11

wí·-dú·-da-ł·ákwi·-dú·-tsá·u.

Whoever goes by there he kills.

12

wí·-tłətc-gwa-dɛ́·məł,

But the little fellow (said),

13

ú·. g̣wáłɣi-wantł kwə-'nəx̣-wáł-'walu

Oh I will rip him up with my knife

14

áɴ-dji-ditc iłdí·yɛ'wɛ

But do not say so

15

má·tsi dłú·g̣wi.

Do just remain here.

16

áɴ-g̣ɛ́ʻ-łá'yáq̓ai

Do not go there

17

tsúwɛ·-nántł.

He will kill you.

18

ú·· an-wántł tsúwɛ·n.

Oh he will not kill me.

19

tsú-ayú·-łá,

And then indeed he went,

20

łá·-dúhwiyɛ́·t.

he ran as he went.

21

hɛ́·i̓ g̣ɛ́ndji mɛ́·t'cʻnə

Hey! where are you going?

22

ú·qʷcidjɛ-mɛ́·t'c'u.

Oh I am going south.

23

h̓ú an·ántł-g̣ɛ́ʻ-dji.

Humph! you will not get there.

24

tsú-g̣·ałám tłə-x̣-wá·-dɛ́·məł,

Then the big man seized him,

25

sɛ́łu-g̣á-lám,

he grabbed his head,

26

tsú-hantł-tsá·u.

and now he was going to kill him.

27

tsú-má·tsi-hídji kwí-tg̣ántłts-hɛ́łú,

But then he stabbed him first,

28

áyú-tsx̣á·-du'wɛ́ʻ,

indeed he split open his belly,

29

áyu-kwi·-tsá·u tłɛ-hɛthɛ́·dɛ.

indeed he killed the wealthy (giant) headman.

30

tsú-má·tsi-bí·nát's tsú-wɛ́·st

So then he turned back (north again) and reached home.

31

tsá·u'u- kwə-hɛthɛ́·dɛ.

I have killed that wealthy headman.

32

tsú-da-x̯-dik̓íł·ɛ-ditc-ayá·djə

Now you must have done something pretty good indeed

33

mɛ'mɛ́·yuł-hántł.

Now they will come and war on us.

34

á·yu dji'níyam-də-k̓aʻ,

Indeed people (intermediaries) arrived,

35

k̓wɛ́·ndjindji·nu.

they brought news.

36

án-tcíł-tshantł djí·-xałtá·m

We will do you no harm,

37

i-tci·ł-tshantł-sk̓át.

if you pay (for our headman's death).

38

k̯̓ɛ́·łɛ.

Very well.

39

sk̓idíya·ł-hántł.

We will pay you.

40

náim-ił-án-dú·ha'ya tłi-iłdɛ-hɛthɛ́·dɛ,

because they had not liked their (murdered) headman,

41

náim-áɴ-wɛ·n.

because he had been mean.

42

á·yu yu-hántł-tłə-k̓wɛ́·yɛ'is'ədjɛkwi-tcíłnəha-dái'məs,

Now if indeed you reach the mountain with your money

43

wí· gu·s-dítc-hantł̯-k̓ɛ́·lɛ.

then everything will be all right.

44

wí-yə-hántł-áɴ,

But if you will not pay so large a sum,

45

wí·-tłdjíłda·mitcin-hántł.

then we will fight you.

46

á·kyu-iłkán·itłi-iłdahadái'məs,

Indeed they tried (measured) then money,

47

áɴ-gɛ́ʻ dit'cítcdəs tłi-iłdahadái'məs.

but their money was not long enough (to reach) there.

48

í-tcil·antkł-án·sk̓at má·-idzí·məs wúłmɛuł-hantł.

If you will not pay it we will wage war next year.

49

ú· g̣áłamł-hántł t̓ə́ma kwa-hadái'məs.

Oh we will obtain that money by that time.

50

tsú-iłwə́s·i.

Now they went back home.

51

tsu-má···tsiya wudłám·aq-tłgwə́łs-iłdzí·ya,

Now indeed they made large canoes,

52

t'cíc-djɛ-hɛ́·n ił-kwi·-dzí·ya,

far back in the woods they made them,

53

án-i·dax̣-wí-kwi·-k̓wá·ni'yadáʻ.

so that no one would know.

54

g̣á·ł-tłgwə́łs-iłhu·-t'súwa.

They finished a large number of canoes.

55

tsú-hí·hi'yu-dðə'idzí·məs tsú-ił·á,

Now when the appointed time of year came they went,

56

ma·-gúm-an-g̣ɛ́ʻ-dji-dit'cítcdis tłi-iłdahadái'məs.

but the length of their money did not reach to there.

57

g̣ɛnt'cín·si g̣ahá·is iłnántł-hís-níya'mi,

We will give you five days,

58

ínantł-áɴ-dji·ná·-t̓a wɛ́·s-dət'cítcdis,

(and) if you do not come with such a length (of money),

59

wí-wúł·mɛuł-hántł.

then we will war.

60

k̯̓ɛ́·łɛ.

Very well.

61

dzə́nwauł-hántł.

We will bring it to you.

62

wí-an-i ł-máʻ-wɛ́·n.

But they did not do that.

63

má·tsi-ił-gú·s-táix̣áya tłi-iłdətłgwə́łs,

They merely took their canoes down into the water,

64

tsú-ił-má·tsi-nɛ́qɛ,

and they simply fled,

65

báłdi·mí·cdja iłsə́łt̓əts.

they went out to the ocean.

66

wí-idjáu-ił-du·-yú·'yu,

And wherever they halted,

67

ma-íł-du-x̣g̣ɛ́t-xwutxwí·nu.

they were driven away from that place notwithstanding.

68

yuwút's-du·-hít'ci-idzí·məs-da-dłɛ́·g̣ɛq,

Sometimes they would remain an entire year,

69

tsu-du-xwutxwí·nu'umɛ,

and then they would be driven away,

70

náim-ił-áɴ-wɛ́·n.

because they were mean.

71

wɛ́·n-má···-bałdi·micdja-kwi-iłła'áyam.

Then they went away to an entirely different ocean.

72

wí-tłə-nətsłɛ·nɛ́·-k̓aʻ g̣ɛ́sdɛ́ʻ-dji níyám,

Now the moving people (the Whites) came to here,

73

wí·-x̣-kwi·-hit'ci yuxwúna tłɛ-k̓á',

and they took with them one of the (Miluk) Indians,

74

wí·-g̣ɛ́ʻ-kwi·-djíya'naʻ tłɛ-x̣-n̥tsłɛ́·nɛ́·-k̓a tłə-k̓áʻ,

the moving people took an ( an Indian to there ) Indian to there,

75

tłə-n̥t'cbáya·-k̓á dət'łdá-a·sədjə.

to the country where the braided hair (japanese) people

76

wi·-tú·'mət'ł,

Now one old man (there),

77

łə́n··wi-tú·'mət'ł,

an exceedingly aged man,

78

wí·-x̣-míl·ugwi-kw̯í·-k̓xidíya'yɛis tłɛ-x-sə'gandá·s.

this se<ganda:<s man (there) spoke in Miluk to him.

79

wɛ́·n-íł·at.

This is what he said.

80

ɛ́n·ɛ wú-tła gwí·dɛ·t.

I am the only one left of the older generation.

81

tłə-kwí-yimł-hí·mɛ tłə-ságandá·s.

I was the child of the se<ganda:<s.

82

x̣-wɛ́·n-g̣wəsg̣wái tłɛ-x̣-sə́gandá·s.

That is what that se<ganda:<s said to him.

83

wí·-wɛ́·n-ditc tłə-gwí-k̓wá·-'nⁱyada tłi-ił-g̣ɛ́ʻ-yú·yu tłə-sə́gandá·s tłə-n̥t'cbáya-k̓á tłə-dət'łdáya·sədjə.

That is why they know that the se<ganda:<s stopped there in the country of the braided hair people.

84

gú·s-kwi-g̣wəsg̣wái tłə-k̓áʻ tłə-sə́gandá·s tú·'mət'ł łə́n·wi tú·'mət'ł.

The aged se<ganda:<s man told the ( Indian everything) Indian everything.

85

wí·-gu·s-kwi-k̓wa·ná·na'ya djí-kwi-łag̣áwiya·t̓anidiya tłi-iłdəx̣-má·ni'ya·s,

He told all that ( his parents had told ) his parents had told him,

86

tłi-ił-g̣ɛ́ʻcyə́ɣ·tcu.

(when) they had gone all over there.

87

wá·g̣a-t̓ə́m·i'itc-iłg̣ɛ́łts.

We crossed a great river.

88

wí·-dá-qłə́mniyu wí·-sú·du-tłə-báłdí·məs,

But after that the ocean changed,

89

má·tsi-wug̣adíya,

and then it got bigger,

90

bíł·gwətc báldi·məsíya,

91

yə-kwí·-tú·ya-tłə-t'łdá·yas,

92

bíł·-gwətc-kwi·-wí·du.

93

wí-má·tsi-báłdi·'mis-g̣ɛ́ʻ-tsu·m.

Now it is just ocean there.

94

wí·-x̣-wɛ́·n-ditc də-wɛ·-g̯ɛ́ ʻdjimín'niyɛ.

That is why it is impossible to cross there now.

95

x̣-wɛ́·n g̣wəsg̣wái-tłɛ-x̣-sə́gandá·s tłə-k̓áʻ.

That is what the se<ganda:<s told the Indian.

96

mí·t'cíła-də-k̓áʻ-'n̥tłí·s.

I am the only one left who can talk the people's language

97

yu-wantł-q̓áyau án-hántł-wíʻ-kwí·-də-tłí·s-diu.

98
