How a child was frightened, and later taught to be fearless

72 lines · from Melville Jacobs’s Coos texts, dictated by Annie Miner Peterson (1933–34); keyed 1986–1990.

1

tłɛ-hɛ́·niyɛ i-du-k̓·ílga áɴ-wɛ·n,

Long ago when a child was not good ( mean ),

2

gu·s-mín-áx̣ats,

it cried continually,

3

wi/-wɛn-ił-du-íldwa,

they would speak thus to it,

4

qá'wí·-du

Be silent

5

qanúdj á-nántł-titsá·mi inantł-áɴ-ɛ́·wi áx̣ats.

I will throw you outside if you do not stop crying.

6

wí-x̣ú·t'łuc nantł-g̣álmídzun.

Then a dangerous thing will take you.

7

wí-yu-máʻ-gu·s-mín-áx̣ats,

And if nevertheless it continued to cry,

8

tí·ts-qánu·dja

Throw him outside

9

wɛ́n-ił-du-íldwa.

That is what they would say.

10

x̣-ɛ́q-hántł-kwi·-g̣álam.

Dead people will take him.

11

wɛ́n-ił-du-aq'álqsit̓úwa i-du-áiwɛ-ɛ́·k̓

That's way they frightened them when they were still little.

12

wí-tłuwa-ɛ́n·ɛ,

When I myself was a (child),

13

wí-wɛ́·n-ił'u wɛ́·tsin-du.

that is what they did to me.

14

ax̣átswu-du.

I used to cry.

15

hɛ́ ⁱ-má·tsi-káwu'la ditc-dɛ́·djɛ wí-x̣-kwi-u/g̣álmídzun,

Then some thing ( some thing ) with a ( a basket ) basket took me,

16

wí-tłə-dək̓há'adjá'u gíla·dzun·

and she put me into her basket.

17

wí-á·yu x̣-wɛ́·n-ni'lúwɛ,

Indeed I thought,

18

á·yú-dax̣ x̣-ɛ́q tłə-ug̣álmidzun.

I guess it really is a dead person who has taken me.

19

wí-hít'cí-hú'mik̓·,

Now one old woman,

20

yi-bi/n·ícdj ɛʻ-dji tłɛ/-ɛ́q ɛ́n·ɛ́-də́t̓iᴍ,

when the dead person with me as its pack got to the door,

21

wí-kwi-hwə́ldi-tłətc-hú'mik̓·.

that old woman jumped up.

22

hɛ́-hɛ́·niyɛ-tłə-naq̓áyau

Oh you died long ago

23

x̣-wɛ́n-íldᵘwa.

That is whst she said.

24

í·g̣ɛⁱ

Go away

25

áɴ-t'swá'a

Let her alone

26

dɛ́·wún-hantł áxats

She will stop crying.

27

tsú-wɛ́-x̣g̣ɛt-wɛ·tíndzu·n.

Then she took me away from that.

28

wí-wɛ·n-'ï'lúwɛ.

This is what I thought.

29

á·yu-da-ɛ́q,

That must have been a dead person,

30

ná·yím-du tg̣wə́nwə́sədjɛ g̣ɛ́ʻ-du-łu sik̓·ínyim.

because such fastened things are in graveyards.

31

á·yu-da-x· ɛ́q,

And so it surely must have been a dead person,

32

náyim-du ká·wəl g̣ɛʻ-sik̓·ínyim tłə-tg̣wə́nwə́sədjɛ.

because a pack basket was fastened there in the graveyard.

33

x̣-wɛ́n-u kwí·-tłq̓á·ya.

That was what I believed.

34

hɛ́ⁱ-x̣-kwə-tłí·ł ɛ́n·ɛł-nə'yɛ́·dzitc dlú·g̣wa-ditc.

But indeed she was merely one who lived in our own house.

35

án-x̣-kwə-ɛ́q yá.

She was no dead person at all.

36

k̓á·-x· tsi-aq'álqsít̓aⁱ

It was only a person who had terrified me.

37

tłɛ-hɛ́·niyɛ-hí·mɛ i-du-ha -́'wíyam gwa-t̓í·cdji'iyɛ iłdi'idzí·mis,

Long ago when children grew up to about the age of ten years,

38

an-íł-du-dá·s aq'álqsit̓uwa.

they did rot frighten them any more.

39

anyá-du-idzɛ́·wədjə́mɛ.

They no longer stayed inside.

40

qánu-du-kwí-án-hú·dzi·da.

They were outside doing things.

41

x̣-wɛ́·n-ił-du há·'wí·t̓a.

That is the way they raised them.

42

wí-á·yú-du áɴ-ditc álqsa,

Then indeed there was nothing they would be afraid of,

43

t'cɛ́ʻ-du-kwi-yú·dɛt,

they would travel far back in the woods,

44

gú·s-dítc wí-tłɛ́-ił-kwi·-k·łá·wi,

they would see everything (tracks, signs) quickly (readily),

45

náim-ił-gú·s-míɴ łú·dədá-ya tłi-nəbɛ́lɛx̣ɛ-ditc.

because they were always watching the wild things (beasts).

46

x̣-wɛ·'n-ditc tłi-ił-gu -́s-míɴ gu-s-dítc-łú·dədaya,

That is why they watched everything all the time,

47

náim-iłdú·ha'ya k·łá·wi-tłə-máʻ-ditc.

because they wanted to (find and) see such things.

48

x̣-wɛ́n-ditc tłi-ił-gu·s-dítc-mit'ssíyada,

That is why they knew (recognized) everything,

49

gu·s-dítc-ił k̓·ílɛ-łú·dədáya

they watched everything carefully,

50

tłə-dítc-dá-hag̣ádi.

for tracks of things.

51

mái-an-łín -wi mít'cʻyu,

Even though it did not show strongly (markedly),

52

ma-íł-kwi·-k·łá·wi,

they would see it anyway,

53

náim-x̣-má·-ni yás-də x̣-wɛ́·n-mit'smít'stiya tłə-nuk̓wí·n.

because it was their parents who had taught them thus about the woods.

54

wí·-gu·s-íł-kwi·-mitá·i,

They noticed everything,

55

áyu-i-dáʻ-ditc-di-yuxwúmtaʻ,

and indeed if something had been about there,

56

wí-k̓wá·'niyadá'ił í-dítc tłə-dá ʻ-yuxwúmɛ tłə-áɴ-wɛ·n-ditc.

they would know if whatever had been there was no good (was ""mean'').

57

yúwút's-łí·tcit,

It might have been a cougar,

58

yuwút's-bátgi,

it might have been a bobcat,

59

yuwút's nɛk·'ɛ́lɛhɛ́-k̓aʻ.

it might have been a person-who-calls-out.

60

x-wɛ́n-ił-du-mít'smit'stíya tłi-ił·dihí·mɛ.

That was how they taught their children.

61

á·yu-i'ł-du kwí·-mit'ssá·ts.

Then indeed they learned what was to be done.

62

máⁱ-aɴ-łín·wi-mít'cʻyu,

Even though it did not show up strongly,

63

ma-íł-du-k̓wá·'niyá·da dítc-tłə-dáʻ yuxwúmɛ.

they knew nevertheless what thing had been about there.

64

wɛ́·n-ił-du-mit'smít'stiya tłí-iłdihí·mɛ.

That is the way they taught their children.

65

á·yú-du i-hɛ́·wi,

Then when they were grown up,

66

wí-aɴ-má·tsi-ła-dəhwíyɛ·t,

they would not just run along,

67

gu·s-dítc-du·-g·ɛ́ mitá·i.

but they would observe everything.

68

x̣-wɛ́n-tłi-iłha·'wí·ta tłi-iłdihí·mɛ.

That is the way they raised their children.

69

wí-i-hɛ́·wi wí-aɴ-ditc-álqsa,

When they had grown up they feared nothing,

70

náim-k̓wá·'niyáda gú·s-kwi-mit'ssíyada tłə-máʻ-ditc dəhá-g̣adi.

because they knew all that had been taught them about tracks of things.

71

wɛ́·n-ił-du tłə-kwí-aɴ-idzɛ́wtc-idzí·m,

That is why they did not stay at home,

72

náim-ił-áɴ-ditc-álqsa.

because there was nothing they feared.
