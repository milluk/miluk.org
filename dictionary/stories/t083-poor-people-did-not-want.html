Poor people did not want

37 lines · from Melville Jacobs’s Coos texts, dictated by Annie Miner Peterson (1933–34); keyed 1986–1990.

1

tłɛ-hɛ́·niyɛ́-k̓aʻ,

The people of long ago,

2

aɴ-dú-huwɛ tłə-kwí·'nɛ'wɛ́t'ł k̓áʻ-dihí·mɛ tłə-du-kwí-án- x̣-wi-hámaq̓áma náim-ił-áɴ-dú·há'ya tłə-dɛx̣-má·ni'yas.

the reason the children of poor persons were never seen was because their parents did not want it ( them to be seen by richer people).

3

yuwút's̯-x̣-k̓ílɛ́·-k̓áʻ kwí·-k̯łá·wi,

Perhaps if a good (well-to-do) person saw her,

4

wí-kwi-iłdú·há'ya.

then they would want her.

5

wí-án-x̣-dji tłə-dɛx̣-má·ni'ya·s yu-kwí-g̣ɛʻ-ní·da·s,

Then their parents would not have to give her there,

6

náim-ił-kwí'nɛ'wɛt'ł ámi-ił-ditc,

because they were poor and propertyless,

7

an·ántł-djíʻ-kwí-iłdəhɛ́l· ɛ́·q̓ɛs tłə-hɛthɛ́·dɛ-k̓aʻ.

they would not have very wealthy people for in-law family.

8

wí-yuwút's-du-má·tsi-g̣ɛ́ndji ma-íł-du-idjáu,

Sometimes they would just stay around there they lived,

9

dáʻ-ił-du-lɛqlút̓tsim,

because they didn't want rich persons to desire their child.

10

náim-ił-áɴ-dú·ha'ya x̣-hɛthɛ́·dɛ-k̓á kwi·-dú·-há'ya tłí-iłdi̯k̓íłga áɴ-míɴ kwí-idzɛ́wtc tłi-iłdi̯k̓ílga,

Their child would never be inside the house ( of theirs for child),

11

náim-ił-wɛ́·n báhán·a'na·ya,

because they gave her this sort of advice,

12

kwí·'nɛ-'wɛt'ł-ł.

We are poor.

13

an·ántł̯-k̓íl·ɛ-k̓á k̯łú·du·n.

Good (well-to-do) people should not see you.

14

áyu-du-áɴ-idzɛ́·wtc tłə-kwí'nɛ-'wɛt'ł-k̓áʻ tłə-dihí·mɛ.

Indeed children of poor people were never in the house.

15

yuwút's psínł,

Maybe three,

16

yuwút's ádzu kwí-íł-du-ɛ́ik̯it'cín·u tłi-hí·mɛ,

maybe two of children would chum together

17

wí-á·yu án-x̣-wi-kwi-hámáq̓ama tłɛ-g̣ɛ́nɛ́tc-k̓aʻ.

and then indeed those young women never would be seen.

18

wí-yuwút's x̣-gɛ́ndji-də́-k̓aʻ,

Sometimes people would come from somewhere,

19

yuwút's-x̣-g̣úg̣ws,

perhaps from Lower Coquille River,

20

yuwút's x̣-bɛ́·l,

perhaps from the north,

21

á·yú-du yuwút's-il-du-tłú·tlúyu x̣-kwí'nɛ'wɛt'ł-k̓a'áma.

and then they would purchase one of those poor people ( daughters).

22

áyú-ił-du-g̣ɛ-hɛhɛ́mildju,

Then they would deliver her to there,

23

an-dú-x̣-wi-kwi-k̓wá·'níyada má·n-du-hɛhɛ́mildju.

and no one would know that she had been taken already.

24

tsə-du-tsú-daʻ-kwi·-k̓wɛ́·niyɛ.

It would be too late when they learned it.

25

x̣-wɛ́·n-tłə-hɛ́·niyɛ́-k̓aʻ.

That is how the people of long ago (were).

26

an-dú-húwɛ tłə-kwí·-wɛ́·n'nə́mɛ,

That is why they were like that,

27

náim-ił-du yuwút's páu-kʷts tłɛ-x̣-hɛthɛ́·dɛ-k̓aʻ.

because sometimes very rich people would enslave her.

28

x̣-wɛ́·n-ditc tłi-kwí-án-x̣-wi hámaq̓áma tłə-kwí-'nɛ'wɛt'ł-k̓aʻ dihí·mɛ,

That is why no one saw the girls ( the girls) of poor people,

29

náim yuwút's-hɛthɛ́·dɛ-k̓a kwi--dú·ha'ya,

because a very rich person might want them,

30

wí-tłi-iłdɛx̯-hɛ́lɛ'yu hú·mis kwí-iłhú·da-t.

and then his first wives would ridicule her.

31

x̣-wɛ́·n-ditc tłɛ-x̣-kwí'nɛ'wɛt'ł-k̓aʻ kwí·-an-du·'ha'ya hɛthɛ́·dɛ-k̓á ildədɛ́·mił.

That is why poor people did not want very rich people for their husband.

32

yuwút's má·tsi kwi-íłdədzí·yá·t'si.

Sometimes they would merely take her to do their work.

33

wɛ́·n-ditc-tłi-il-kwí áɴ-dú·ha'ya k̯̓ibíndjɛ-ił-kwí· ní·das tłi-ildi̯k̓ílga,

That is why they did not want to give their child to rich people,

34

ámi-iłhadái'mis,

without their (having) money,

35

ámi-iłtɛ́·tc.

without their (having) clothes (themselves).

36

wí-wɛ·n-ditc tłi-ił-áɴ-hɛthɛ́·dɛ'ɛdjɛ kwí-ni'náⁱ tłi-iłdihí·mɛ,

That's why they didn't give their children ( daughters) to rich people,

37

ná·yim yuwút's-ił má·tsi-gwa-hú·da·t.

because sometimes they would only ridicule her.

The voice in these texts is Annie Miner Peterson’s, recorded by Melville Jacobs in 1933–34.
