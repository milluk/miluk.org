A rejected wife and her

59 lines · from Melville Jacobs’s Coos texts, dictated by Annie Miner Peterson (1933–34); keyed 1986–1990.

1

i-du-kʷdlí·yɛn hú·mis wí-lín·wi-du-áɴ̯-k̓ilɛ,

When a woman was unloved that was extremely bad,

2

náim-tłə-də-má·-humɛ̯·k̓ɛ tłə-kwi-yímł-hí·mɛ x̣-gu·s-íł-du áɴ-dú·há'ya.

because the children of the other wives all had nothing to do with it (with the child of the undesired woman).

3

x̣-ɛ́stís tłə-dɛ́·mił dəhí·mɛ an-íłdú·há'ya tłətc-kʷdłí·yɛn k̯̓íłga.

Some husband's kids did not like the unloved (woman) woman's kid.

4

wí·-łínwi-áɴ̯-k̓ɛ́·lɛ,

It was not at all good,

5

náim-tłə-dɛx qat'sá·más hat'łíyadas an-íł-du-dú·ha'ya.

because his half brothers did not want him.

6

áyú-du-g̣ɛ-łínwi áɴ-x̣-wɛ·n.

Indeed such a thing was exceedingly bad.

7

wí-yuwút's-wə́s·i tłə-k̓á-kwi·-də̯k̓ílga.

Sometimes the person who had that child returned home.

8

wí-yɛ-h ɛ́·wi tłə-də'ɛ́n ɛ-dədjínu,

Then when he grew up with his mother's people,

9

wí-an-du·'ha'ya tłə-də'ɛ́ł·ɛ.

he did not like his father.

10

wí-yi̯-k̓ílɛ·-k̓á x̣-wɛ́-n kʷdlí·nɛn tłə-di̯k̓ílga,

And when fine people'd such unloved woman who's their child,

11

wi-wá·-ga-ditc-du.

it was a big (serious) thing.

12

yuwút's-kwi-yɛ́·nu-tsú·tsu tłə-dɛ́·mił,

They might have killed her husband for that,

13

náim-hú·dədá·was iłwá·-wa,

because they made an insult of it,

14

tłə-kwí·-dəkʷdlí·yɛn tłə-di̯k̓ílga,

when it was their child who was unloved,

15

má-his-kúwi-hidjím·ił̯-k̓ílga máʻ-kwi·-hú·da·t-wá·wa.

for he likewise humiliated his own child (and her child).

16

wí-yuwút's kwi-yímł-qdla ⁱ wulmɛ́u,

Then possibly they fought over that,

17

wɛ́·n̯-k̓í·dzímɛu,

and they killed each other,

18

i-ił-x̣-wɛ·n-wawa.

when they did like that to it.

19

náu-yɛ-hɛthɛ́·dɛ-k̓á an-íł-du-náx̣di i-ił-áɴ-dú·há'ya.

When she's wealthy people they didn't touch if they didn't desire her

20

x̣-má du-g̣á-lam tłə-hú·mis.

Another would take the woman.

21

má-x̣-kwi yímł-ditc,

Some other of his own relatives,

22

yuwút's-x̣-hɛt'łɛ́·də,

sometimes his older brother,

23

yuwút's tłə-dɛ'ɛ-łɛ́·-dəmítłgwə́ła-di̯k̓ílga,

sometimes his father's younger brother's child,

24

kuwí-kwiyɛ́·-dəhú·mis.

he would be the one to marry the woman.

25

wí-á·yu̯-k̓ɛ́·lɛ.

Then indeed it would be all right.

26

x̣-wɛ́·n náu-ił-du-áɴ-t'swá·laʟ i-ił-áɴ-dú·há'ya,

That is why they never touched her if they did not want her,

27

náim-áɴ·wɛ-n yɛ-hɛthɛ́·dɛ-di̯k̓ílga.

because it was bad if it was a very wealthy person's child.

28

wɛ́·n-ditc tłi-ił-áɴ-dú·ha'ya kwí-íłdəkʷdlí·yɛn,

That is why they did not want her to be an unloved woman,

29

náim kwi-yímł-qdlaⁱ tsú·-dzímɛu.

because they killed one another for such a thing.

30

yɛ-hɛthɛ́·dɛ-dihí·mɛ yi-wə́s·i,

If a very wealthy person's child returned home,

31

wɛ́n-da-hɛ́·wi tłə-di̯k̓ílga,

and her child grew up there,

32

wí-aɴ-dú-da-mał-dú·há'ya.

she never wanted to marry.

33

wí-i̯-k̓ílɛ-k̓á' kwí·-dúha'ya,

But if good (well-to-do) people wanted her,

34

kwítc-x̣-wɛ́n-lɛ·t'cí·m-k'áʻ hɛt-hɛ́·dɛ,

those whom they called very wealthy people,

35

wí-i-dú·ha'ya tłə-dɛx̣-ɛ́ł·ɛ,

and if her father wished him (too),

36

w̯í-k̓x̯ídíya'yɛi-s tłə-dəgwá·ya.

he would go and talk with his daughter.

37

wí-yu-wá·'nu,

Then if she would say,

38

k̯̓ɛ́·lɛ,

Very well,

39

wí-tsu-t̓ə́ma g̣ɛ́ʻ-k̓áʻ-txwíya tłə-dədɛ́·miłidjɛ,

then they would send a person yonder to her husband,

40

wí-yu-wáʻnu,

and if he would say (also),

41

k̯̓ɛ́·lɛ,

Very well,

42

wí-á·yu mɛ́-x̣-wɛ́·n.

then that is what would be done indeed.

43

wí-tsú-t̓ə́ma-yú·du·nam.

He would be given back his money.

44

wí-á·yu dɛ́mi-tsəm tłə-dənəg̣wíyɛ,

And then indeed his former relative 'd be married again,

45

wí-ni'níyu tłə-dəhadái'mis.

when they had given him back his money.

46

wí-hɛthɛ́·dɛ-k'aʻ,

But a very wealthy person,

47

má'yukʷ-kʷdlí·nan-hú·mis,

even if he did not want the unloved woman any more,

48

wí-aɴ-dú·ha'ya tłə-dəhadái'mis.

would not want (back) his money,

49

á ɴ-du·ha'ya,

He did not want it,

50

x̣-kwí'yu-hú·dɛt tłə-di̯k̓ílga.

lest that humiliate his child (by his former wife).

51

wí-x̣-wɛ́·n-ditc tłə-kwí-aɴ-dú·ha'ya tłə-dəhadái'mis.

That is why he did not want his money (returned).

52

á·yú-du-x̣-kwí'yú wá·ga-ditc tłə-di̯k̓ílga,

It indeed was what made his child big,

53

ya-áɴ-bíyat̓a tłə-dəhadái'mis.

if he did not take back his money.

54

á-yú-du-án-x̣-wíkwí-hú·dat tłə-di̯k̓ílga,

Indeed no one could make light of his child,

55

wí-tsú-tłɛ-x̣-wɛ́·n̯-k̓ílga.

if it was that kind of a child.

56

wí-yuwút's-wə́s·i tłə-dɛ'ɛłɛ́·djintc,

And sometimes it would go back to its father,

57

ha'ní·c-du-dáʻ-dlú·g̣wa.

it would remain there for a while.

58

wí-da·s-bí·nat's ɛ́ndjintcdə.

Then it would return again to its mother.

59

x̣-wɛ́n-tłə-k̓áʻ-dətá·má·łis.

That was the custom of the people.

The voice in these texts is Annie Miner Peterson’s, recorded by Melville Jacobs in 1933–34.
