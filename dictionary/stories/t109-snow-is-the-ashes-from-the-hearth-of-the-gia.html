Stories

Snow is the ashes from the hearth of the giant spider

8 lines · from Melville Jacobs’s Coos texts, dictated by Annie Miner Peterson (1933–34); keyed 1986–1990.

1

x̣-wɛ́·n-du-lagáwiya·da·ní·da tłɛ́-k̓áʻ i-du-sdl'líyam.

This is how the Indians accounted for it when it snowed.

2

'ə́̓ tsú-dá-x̣-gúm x̣t̓á·-dík̯tsa·s tłɛ-x̣-wá·-wá'at'ł,

Oh! I guess the giant spider is throwing (shoveling) out his ashes,

3

sdl'líyam-gum.

now it is snowing.

4

x̣-wɛ́·n-ił-du-íldⁿwa tłɛ́-x̯-k̓áʻtłə́-sdlá·lis.

That is how the people spoke about the snow.

5

tsú-da-x̯-gum t'łcá·-dík̯tsa·s tłɛ-x̣-wá·-wá'at'ł.

I suppose giant spider is sweeping out his ashes.

6

wɛ́n-ił-du-íldⁿwa tłə-sdlá·lis tłɛ́-x̣-k̓áʻ,

That is the way the people spoke of the snow,

7

i-du-sdł'líyam.

when it snowed.

8

gwá·-x̯-tsá·-du wa-wá-'at'łdə k̯tsá·s.

They said it was the ashes of the giant spider.

The voice in these texts is Annie Miner Peterson’s, recorded by Melville Jacobs in 1933–34.
