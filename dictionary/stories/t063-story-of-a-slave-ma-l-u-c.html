Story of a slave, maál=uúc

51 lines · from Melville Jacobs’s Coos texts, dictated by Annie Miner Peterson (1933–34); keyed 1986–1990.

1

tłɛ-hɛ́·niyɛ wí-kwi-du-pukpú·wak'ił-du tłi-hí·mɛ.

They enslaved children long ago.

2

wí-kwi-tłə-dí·lusł wí-ku-ig̯ɛ́·k̯ yu-kwi-pú·kwiya

There was diu who was one those who were taken for slaves.

3

tłɛ-hɛ́·niyɛ tłí·-·-du-pú·kwiyu.

Long ago we were taken for slaves.

4

wí-kwi-pú·kwiyu'u,

I was enslaved,

5

wí·-x̣-wɛ́·n wuhithí·tɛq̓hɛm.

and that was how they kept on selling me.

6

wí·-g̣ɛ́ʻ udjíndji·nu tlə-má·łú·cidja.

They took me yonder to the Columbia River.

7

wi-dú·ha'ya'u-du nɛ́qhɛ,

I wanted to flee,

8

wí-aɴ-wú-du-x̣-dji-nɛ́qhɛ,

but I could not get away,

9

náim-il gu·s-mín ił'úłú·dədími·du·n.

because they watched me continually.

10

gú·s-míɴ mái-ł-gu·s-ditc-x̣áłi,

All the time it mattered not what sorts of things we did,

11

wi-máʻ-íł-wu-du-lú·dədími·du·n

they watched me all the same.

12

mínt'ci tłi-ił-g̣ɛ́jyɛq-hɛ-łdlím·aʟ.

Once we were spearing their salmon.

13

an-íłmit'ssíyada-tłə-'n̥tłi· ́s,

They did not know my language (Coos),

14

wɛ́n-tłi-iłhidjím·ił-tłí·s

and I did not know their language (Chinook).

15

an-úmit'ssíyada.

When they came back,

16

wí-ił-kwi·-wɛ́·st,

other young persons were playing,

17

wí-má·-hí·mɛ alicá·ní·da,

and they sang songs in their language.

18

wí-kwi-há·-di·da tłi-iłmí·disa·ma iłdimɛg̣ɛ́·n wí-wɛn- iłhá·t̓i. gayá gaya gayá, sɛmɛli·'i sɛmɛli·'i ɛhɛ'ɛ́i ɛhɛ'ɛ́i.

This is how they sang. gayá gayá gayá semelii'i s emelii'i, ɛhɛ'ɛ́i ɛhɛ'ɛ́i.

19

tsú·x̣-wɛ-n-'nɛ'lúwɛ tłə-ɛ́n·ɛ,

Then I thought to myself,

20

tsú-wi-his-ɛ́n·ɛ-g̣ɛhát̓í·wa.

Now I will sing too.

21

iłkí·'u í·djɛ'ɛ́i-g̣ɛ́ʻ-ła,

They made me go there when I was poor,

22

ɛ́n·ɛ t'łí du·-wu̯-k̓ɛ́·lɛ-dzə́nɛ

But I am returning well off.

23

g̣ɛ́-iłwut'cú·lí·du.

They stared there at me.

24

tsú-iłg̣wí·'ɣiyam tłi-iłdəx̣-mí·disa·ma nəɣálá.

They talked together in their language.

25

x̣-wɛ́n tlu-wu-dáʻ-ídjɛ.

That is what it was like when I was there.

26

mín·t'ci tsu-ánya-wułú·didími·tɛ·m.

Then once they were not watching me.

27

wí-tsu-wun ɛ́qhɛ.

I ran away.

28

qłím-wú-du-yuxwúmɛ.

I traveled at night.

29

wí-i-g̣ɛ́ł·im wí-ni̯k̓ín dít'łha'wáya wu-du-tsí·m

When it became daylight I would be beside a log.

30

wí-yu-wú-du-cit'cdí·djɛʻ-dji,

When I reached a bay,

31

wí-tsú·-du-gú·s-wí tsú·m,

and they had all gone to bed,

32

tsú-wu-du-tłgú·s-ła·'ya,

I would steal a canoe,

33

wí-yu-wú-du-hí·q̓at,

and when I landed,

34

wí-t·ca'wú-du tłə-tłgú·s,

I would shove off the canoe,

35

á·yú-du-tłx̯ú.

so that it drifted away.

36

wí-x̣-wɛ·n an-íł-k̓wá·niyada x̣-djí-tłə-kwi áɴ-ditc-tłi-iłgú·s.

Then they would not learn how their ( tgus) canoe had disappeared.

37

wí-tkłə-dət'łə́hɛ tá·'wú-du.

I would throw away the paddle.

38

wí-yuwút's-du̯-k̓íyas n̥t'łɛ́hɛ,

I might have only a small stick for my paddle,

39

kwí·-wu-du-g̣áł·áts.

with which to go across.

40

x̣-wɛ́·n-tłə-wútsin'wɛ́q̓hɛm.

That is the way I came back.

41

tsłíᴍ-tłə-wun ɛ́qhɛ,

I fled in summer,

42

á·yú-wu-yúkʷsíʟ-dlá·ᵘ.

so I ate fruits and berries.

43

k̓á'-di'yú·pta kwi-wú'yityú·wat.

I had Indian fire sticks.

44

wí-yu-wuh ɛ́·yɛ,

When I was at a distance (from the Columbia River),

45

wi-hɛ́·nt'cicdjɛʻ-wú-du,

and I was far inland,

46

wí-yuwút's-t̓ím·i-da'-tsí·m,

and possibly a creek lay (flowed) there,

47

wí-t'łdjíʻ-wú-du-dzá·qʷts.

I would spear trout.

48

wí-á·yú-du-kwi-nə́q̓ʷɴ.

Then that was what I ate.

49

x̣-wɛ́·n-wutsin'wɛ́q̓hɛm,

That is the way I came back,

50

wɛ'n-x̣-wɛ-n-u'wɛ·'st.

that is how I returned home.

51

wí-wɛ́·n-ditc tłu-u-wɛ́·n sín·sá·nu má·łú·c·,

That is why I was named maal=uc (Columbia River).

The voice in these texts is Annie Miner Peterson’s, recorded by Melville Jacobs in 1933–34.
