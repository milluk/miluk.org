The pouty children

94 lines · from Melville Jacobs’s Coos texts, dictated by Annie Miner Peterson (1933–34); keyed 1986–1990.

1

dzúwisəmɛ hí·mɛ

The pouty children

2

tłə-hí·mɛ x̣-ax̯íyax̯itc də-kwí·-dlá·g̣aq.

The children were living with their uncle (mother's brother).

3

mínt'ci tsí-itc bɛ́lɛx̣ɛ-nu.

One time they just became angry at him.

4

í·g̣ɛis-hantł.

We are going to get away from here.

5

á·yu-itc'í·g̣ɛⁱ,

Indeed they left there,

6

hɛ́·nt'cicdji t̓ə́mi-dək̯dá·na'nəsədjə itcłá,

they went far into the interior to the end of the creek,

7

wɛ́n-g̣ɛ-itcdlɛ́·qsəm,

and there they remained,

8

g̣ɛ́-itcləqlú·t̓tsəm.

there they lived.

9

ha·'wíyam'itc.

They grew up.

10

gu·s-dítc-du-yúg̣wa tłə-x̣-gwɛ́is,

The girl dug all sorts of things,

11

wɛ́n-tłə-dəhɛ́t'łɛ wí·-t'shɛ́yɛ'nu·du łə́m·daidu,

and her older brother trapped and hunted,

12

q̓wə́n-yau-du-łú·-dəda·ya,

he looked after the food,

13

g̣ɛ́lyɛq,

the salmon,

14

kwi-ítc-du·-t'c'lái.

which they dried.

15

á·yu-g̣á·ł-itcdəq̓wə́n·yau,

They certainly had a quantity of food,

16

g̣á·ł-itcdətɛ́·tc,

many garments,

17

gú·s-didjɛ́·nən-itcdag̣a·ł.

quantities of all sorts of things.

18

gu·s-mín-du·-k̯ímɛ·t'ł yúg̣wa tłɛ-x̣-gwɛ́is

The girl dug skunk cabbage all the time.

19

gwa-da-níct'ci-idzí·mis dá-itcdlɛ́·gɛq.

They lived there so many (a considerable number of) years.

20

mín·t'ci áɴ-wɛ·st.

One time she did not come back.

21

g̣atq̓áidiya áiwa-kwi-aɴ-wɛ́·st tłə-gwɛ́is.

Night came but the girl never got back home.

22

a'máis tsu-wúlwa'ya tłə-dəx̣-hɛ́t'łɛ,

The next day her older brother hunted for her,

23

áɴ-g̣ɛ́n·-dji-kwi̯-k̓íl·dᵘwa

but he could not find her anywhere.

24

g̣ɛnt'cínsuya g̣aháis áiwa-aɴ̯-k̓íl·dᵘwa

Even after five days he had not found her.

25

adzú·-x̣g̣ɛyɛ'ɛyɛ áiwa-aɴ̯-k̓íl·dᵘwa

Even after seven he had not found her.

26

gu·s-mín-g̣ahá·ya kwi-wúlwa'ya.

He sought her every day.

27

hɛi-má·tsi g̣wá·n-ditc k̯łá·wi,

Now he saw something up above,

28

wí·-kwi-hámaq̓ dzə́nɛ,

he watched it coming,

29

wí-máʻ-g̣ɛ́ʻ-łú·dəda·ya,

he kept on watching it,

30

hɛⁱ-má·tsi łə́ni̯k̓ídjɛ,

and then it got behind brush,

31

kwi·-áɴ-ditc,

it went out of sight,

32

tsú-g̣ɛ́-ł·á.

and then he went to there.

33

hɛⁱ-má·tsi gwá-nətłp̓íyɛ-ditc.

It was some sort of winged thing (bird).

34

tsú-kwi-wəs·á·ya,

So he took it back home,

35

idzú·djɛ kwí·-tá.

he threw it into the house.

36

tsú-wɛn-di'lúwɛ,

Then he thought,

37

kwi·-'nántł-cí·t̓a.

I will have it for my pet.

38

g̣ɛnt'cínsu'ya g̣aháis máʻ-daʻ-kwi·-dlú·g̣wa,

After five days it was still there,

39

hís-gúma-áɴ-lix̯lí·x̯,

it never even moved at all,

40

má·tsi-dáʻ-dlú·g̣wa

it just sat there.

41

hit'cí-q̓ɛłí·'mis hɛⁱ-má·tsi-áɴ-ditc

One morning (later) it was gone.

42

má·-dlá-nan tłi-bíndj má·-kwi-áɴ-ditc

Even though the door had been shut it was gone.

43

hɛⁱ-má·tsi k̓áʻ-dɛ́·djɛ,

Now then a person entered,

44

tłax̣tłá·wax̣-dəhá·mis.

switching the water out of his hair with his hand.

45

sdlɛ́·q̓-dáx̯ tłə-k̓áʻ

That person must have been in swimming.

46

tsú-kwi·-dɛ́·djɛ,

Now he entered,

47

tsú-dlúqʷsəm.

and he sat down.

48

x̣-wɛ́n·ɛ'lúwɛ́nðtłp̓íyɛ-ditc-ú.

You suppose that I am a winged thing (a bird).

49

aɴ-ú-nətłp̓í-yɛ-ditc,

I am not a winged thing,

50

k̓á'-u.

I am a person.

51

x̣úg̣u wutxwíya

They sent me from up above.

52

x̣-wɛ́n-ditc du-wu-díyu.

That is why I have come.

53

dá'-tłə-nə-kwɛ́·nɛ́t'ł hɛthɛ́·dɛ kwí·-duhá·mis.

The head man there has married your sister.

54

nɛ́u-ɛnəłá·dzidá·mi

I have come to get you.

55

łá·s-hantł.

So let us go.

56

ín·antł-g̯íl·a·tsəm tsí·m-nantł,

When you get in you will lie down,

57

wí-dlúkʷdðədzá·mi-nantł.

and I will cover you over.

58

wí-án·ántł-x̯í·la.

You must not look.

59

tsú-nantł-wɛ́·n-il·dá·mi,

When I tell you so,

60

tsú-nantł-x̯í·la.

then you may look.

61

á·yú-mɛ-x̣-wɛ́·n

Indeed he did so.

62

tsú-g̯íl·a·-tsəm tłə-dí·lú·ł,

The young man got into it,

63

hɛⁱ-má·tsi gwa-hwə́l·di'itc,

and then it was as if they flew,

64

tsú··-itcła

and now they went away.

65

k̯̓ínau-di'lúwɛ tłə-dí·lúł,

The young man became tired of it (of lying covered there),

66

á·iwa-itclá'a

but still they continued going.

67

tsú-itc tsú-wɛ·n-íl·dᵘwa,

Now he told him thus,

68

kwíya-x̯í·la'á·yəx̣.

You may look.

69

á·yu x̯í·la tłə-dí·lúł,

Indeed the young man looked,

70

hɛl-má·tsi t'łdá·yasədjə x̯í·la

and he saw a (large) village.

71

ɛ́·g̣ɛ-nantł-ła,

You are to go yonder,

72

kwítc-ɛ·yɛ́·dzədjə dáʻ-tłə-nəkwɛ́·nɛ́t'ł.

your sister is in that house.

73

g̣ɛ́ʻ-nantł-łá.

You are to go to there.

74

á·yú-g̣ɛ́ʻ-łá-tłə-dí·łúł,

So the young man went there,

75

á·-yú-k̯łá·wi tłə-dəkwɛ́·nɛ́t'ł.

and sure enough he saw his sister.

76

hɛ́···niyɛ-dáʻ-dlú·g̣wa

He remained a long long time at that place.

77

tsú-du-kwi·-hámáq̓ ł'yú'líyam tłə-k̓áʻ gwá-du-nðtłp̓íyɛ kwi-ił'yú'líyam.

And he saw those people travel just like winged things (birds) when they traveled.

78

g̣ɛ́'-ił-du·-g̯íl·a·tsəm

They got into them there.

79

tłi-iłtłgú·s-ditc-dá·-du.

I guess they are their canoes.

80

wí-ił-du·-łá wí-gwa-du-nðtłp̓í yɛ·-ditc hwə́l·di.

When they go they fly just like winged things (birds).

81

tsú-hantł-wə́s·i-tłə-dí·lúł.

Now the young man was going to return home.

82

wɛ́·n-íl·dᵘwa tłə-dəkwɛ́·n ɛ́t'ł,

So he said to his sister,

83

wəs·í-wantł.

I shall go back home.

84

á-yu hɛ́m·ílt'cu.

Indeed they took him home.

85

g̣ɛ́ʻ-lá·liyu,

They put him in there,

86

dá-s-x̣-wɛn g̣ɛʻ-łá·i g̣ɛ́ʻ-ił·á·i'wá·wa tlə-dət'łdáya·sədjə.

87

tsú-g̣ɛʻ-wɛ́·sdu.

88

89

Now he returned to his own people.

90

91

92

93

tsú-dɛ́·wən-g̣ɛ́ndjiʻ-łá.

94
