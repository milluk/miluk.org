Shamans cured tuberculosis

12 lines · from Melville Jacobs’s Coos texts, dictated by Annie Miner Peterson (1933–34); keyed 1986–1990.

1

tłɛ-x̣-wá·-il·áx̣q̓ain,

A strong shaman,

2

wí-tłə-k̓á paqaqáyam,

when a person was continually spitting (from consumption),

3

wí-wɛ́·n-ił-du-íldwa,

his is what they said,

4

i-du-'łxáini tłə-k̓á-x̣ɛ́nwəs.

when he looked at the sick person.

5

tsi-x̣-łú tłə-kwí·-x̣ɛ́nwəs-wá·wa.

It is just this that has made him ill.

6

tłətc-gwáʻ-gú'mɛʻ kwi-dáʻ-dłúwíyam tłə-dəg̣ál·a'yu.

It is like a gu<'me` that is eating on him there inside him.

7

wí·-x̣-kwi· tłɛ-x̣-lux̣láix̣ⁿ-wá·wa,

that is what is making him cough,

8

tłɛ́-x̣-gwáʻ-gu·'mɛ́ʻ-ditc.

that thing like a gu<'me` worm.

9

á·yu-du idzáitst tłə-k̓á łú· dɛx̣ɛ́nwɛ́'ɛs,

Then indeed he worked on the person who had that sickness,

10

wí-i·du·-g̣álám,

and when he took it out,

11

wí·-x̣-gu·s-wí-du·-kʟłá·wi.

they all saw it (the worm).

12

á·yú-du łhɛ́ⁿ tłə-k̓á-x̣ɛ́nwəs.

Indeed then the sick person got well.

The voice in these texts is Annie Miner Peterson’s, recorded by Melville Jacobs in 1933–34.
