Stories

The wife of seal

29 lines · from Melville Jacobs’s Coos texts, dictated by Annie Miner Peterson (1933–34); keyed 1986–1990.

1

qaitsə́n·a-dəhú·mis

The wife of seal

2

gwɛ́is dəq'áimya kwi·-dlɛ́·g̣ɛ·q

There was a girl who lived near the (Coos Bay) bar.

3

wí·-gu·s-mín-du·-kwí·-dzəsdlá·q̓ai.

She swam all the time.

4

hɛi-má·tsi-mínt'ci'itc án-wɛ·st.

and then one time (for a month or two) she did not come back.

5

hɛi-má·tsi í-ił-kwi· íł-kwi·-bánq̓səm tłə-qa'itsə́n·a,

Then (once) when the seals were sunning themselves,

6

hɛi-má·tsi-dá-kwi·-ig̯ɛ́·k̯-tłə-gwɛ́is.

now the girl was there among them.

7

wí·-kwi·-i-ił-k̓áʻ-k̯łá·wi,

When they saw a person (approaching them),

8

wí··nəqaqáyam-tłəl-qáitsə́n·a,

the seals fled,

9

wí·-his-hídji-tłə-gwɛ́is

and also the girl herself.

10

wí·-kwi·-íłk̯imk̯á·m tłə-dɛx̣-má·ni'ya·s.

Now her relatives wept over her.

11

hɛi-má·tsi-hít'ci-q̓ɛłí·'mis tsə-kwí·-wɛ́·st tłə-gwɛ́is.

Then one morning the girl did come back.

12

an-tcílk̯ím·i'í·yəx̣

You must not weep about me.

13

k̓áʻ'ám·á.

They (seals) are people.

14

yɛ́·dzɛdjɛ́·-du·-x̯lə́m·a·,

They go into their houses.

15

wɛ́·n-k̓wɛ·nɛ́·nu yu-kwí-wɛ́·st.

That is what she explained when she came back home.

16

hadái'məs dag̣á·ł wá·sda,

She brought quantities of money (large dentalia),

17

tsú-g̣wálqʷs tłə-dɛ·x̣-dɛ́·məł.

and so (in that way) her husband paid for her.

18

tsú-wə́s·i-dá·s.

Now she went back again.

19

án-hɛ́·niyɛ tsə-nəhí·mɛdɛ-hɛ́mɛqɛ́q̓hɛm.

In no long time they saw her with (seal) babies.

20

wí·-dá·s-wɛ́·st

Now again she came.

21

adzú·n-tła-wɛ́·st,

Only twice did she come back,

22

tsə-dɛ́·wun-wɛ́·st.

and then she never again came home.

23

má-tsiya-du-g̣ɛ́ʻ-hɛmɛqɛ́q̓hɛm i-du-x̯íni'-im tłə-qáitsə́n·a,

And they only saw her (the mother) there when the seals were up on rocks

24

g̣ɛ́ʻ-du-hɛ́m·ɛqɛ́q̓hɛm wí-alica·núdu,

there they saw her playing,

25

tłə-dəhí·mɛ du-ił'alicá·nu

playing with her (seal) children.

26

x̣-wɛ́n mit'sisí·m tłə-kwí·-hidjə́m·ił-hí·mɛ.

That was the way they recognized her and her own children.

27

náim-x̣-wɛ́·n íl·-dᵘwa tłə-dɛx̣-má·ni'ya·s,

Because that was what she had told her parents,

28

náim-x̣-wɛ́·n g̣wəsgwái tłə-dəmá·ni'ya·s.

because that was how she had explained it to her parents.

29

tsú-tsí·-u-wɛ́·s k̓wá·ni'yada ku-kuwí-bá·saq̓

Now that is all I know of that myth.

← Jack Rabbit man Dug-out-of-ground child and popped out of fire →

The voice in these texts is Annie Miner Peterson’s, recorded by Melville Jacobs in 1933–34.
