Stories

Cissy

101 lines · from Melville Jacobs’s Coos texts, dictated by Annie Miner Peterson (1933–34); keyed 1986–1990.

1

tłɛ-hɛ́·niyɛ i-dɛ́·milíyɛ tłə-dədlə́l·wɛ tłə·tcíctcíl-u,

Long ago when the son of tcictcíl·u became a young man,

2

wí-kwi-tłá·wi tłə-Cissy tłə-dədlə́l·wɛ-dəhu ·́misí-dɛ.

he bought Cissy to become the wife of his son.

3

wí-má·tsi áɴ-ha-du -́ha'ya.

But he (the son) did not like her.

4

də́n·ɛ'yu'wú-djɛ íł·a.

They went to the inland people

5

wí-dáʻ hú·mis k̯łá·wi,

There he saw a woman,

6

wí-kuwí-dú·ha'ya.

and she was the one he wanted.

7

wí-wɛ́·n-du-sánsá·-nu tłətc-kúwi-hú·mis ɴancy,

That woman's name was Nancy,

8

x̣-wɛ́·n-disaɴ tłítc-dú·ha'ya hú·mis.

that was the name of the woman he wanted.

9

wí-á·yu-gum kwi·-tłá·wi tłɛ-x̣-tcictcíl·u,

So tcictcíllu purchased her,

10

náim-kwi-dúha'ya tlə-dɛx̣-dlə́l·wɛ.

because his son wanted her.

11

wí-áɴ-dú·ha'ya tłɛ-x̣-ɴancy wí-gu·s-mín kwi·-tłwáhasá·nu.

But Nancy did not want him. She ran away from him continually.

12

wí-má·tsi-t̓ə́ma tłə-n̥tsłɛ́·nɛ́·-k̓aʻ kwi·-dləldjíldu·nis tłə́-k̓aʻ,

At that time the moving people (the whites) fought the people (Indians),

13

wí-dá-qłimniyu hitchit'cú·yú-də-k̓aʻ,

and after that they gathered and drove the people (north),

14

wí-áɴ-dá·s-g̣ɛʻ-ła

so that she did not go there to him again.

15

cílɛ·t'cidjɛ hitchit'cú·yu tłɛ́-k̓áʻ.

They drove the people to Siletz.

16

wí-má·tsí-ya wi-yuqú·n ɛ́djɛ g̣ɛ́-iłláw·a tłə-míl·ukʷ-iłgwəsíya'ił,

But the Lower Coquille Indians went on to Yaquina Bay (from Siletz, where they refused to stay),

17

yuqú·n ɛ́·djɛ-iłláw·a,

they went to Yaquina Bay,

18

wɛ́n-g̣ɛ-ilt'łdáistsim.

there they made their village.

19

wí-má·tsi-gum tcɛ·t̓í-hu·mis g̣alám tłə-dɛx̣-dɛ́·mił tłə-Cissy

Now the husband of Cissy took (without purchasing her) a Chetco woman.

20

psínł-dihí·mɛ,

She (Cissy) had three children (by him),

21

wí-an-dú-x̯ílat tłə-Cissy tłə-dɛx̣-dɛ́·mił.

though her husband did not (hardly ever) look at Cissy.

22

wí-máʻ-da-dlú·g̣wa,

Nevertheless she stayed there,

23

náim-nihí·mɛ́·dɛ.

because she had children by him.

24

wɛ́·n-ditc tłə-kwí-máʻ-da-dlú·g̣wa.

That is why she remained anyway.

25

wí-tłɛ-x̣-dictc-gwaʻ-tcɛ́·t̓i hú·mis hú·da·t-du tłə-Cissy

That sort-of-Chetco woman ridiculed Cissy.

26

mín·t'ci tsɛ-x̣-wɛ́·n-di'lúwɛ,

Once she thought,

27

'mó idɛ-x̣-djí gwɛ-x̣-wɛ́n-ditc uhú·didi·n

Hm! Why does such a thing of a woman make fun of me

28

mánk̯t-wántl

I will give her a beating.

29

mái-ił-wántł máʻ-tsu'wɛ·n,

I do not care if they kill me,

30

gwa-áɴ k̯̓ílɛ·-ditc

the good for nothing

31

wɛ́·n-his-wɛ-x̣-kwi-hú·didi·n

to be ridiculing me like that too

32

tsú-ayú-hwu'ldadza,

Then she leaped at her,

33

tsu-há·misidja-g̣alam,

she grabbed her hair,

34

dłú·t'ci

You stinking thing

35

idjɛ'ɛ́is-hú·mis

you low down woman

36

áminisg̯i dɛ́·-mił dú·ha'ya.

you want a husband for nothing (without marriage payment).

37

nic·á'ɣat̓á·sa

(You with your) cut hair

38

dlú·t'cí-dɛgɛʟ

(with your) bad smelling crotch

39

hís-nɛ-x̣-n ɛ́u hú·didai

and you make fun of me too

40

tsú-hɛ́milt̓idjɛ-tá·ts.

She threw her into the fire.

41

tsú-hántł-qdla tłə-dəhú·mis tłí-itcdɛx̣-dɛ́·mił.

Their husband was going to take his (Chetco) woman's part.

42

tsú-tłə-də' ɛ́n·ɛ-hwə́ldi,

His mother jumped up (remonstrating),

43

áɴ-t'swa'al-kwə-ditc

Don't you bother that poor thing (Cissy)

44

tsú·-x̯-nə-kwí·dit wá·g̣a-wa·wa

And this the sort of thing you think so much of

45

anan-x̣-kwi'yɛ́·nu kwi·-mik̯mán·ak̯ tłə-ditc.

You will not beat the poor thing (Cissy) for that.

46

áɴ-t'swa'al

Don't you bother her

47

an-dá-x̯-nɛ hí·-mɛdidjɛ kwi·-djíndjiná'i

Don't you think something of her because of her children

48

náu-nə-kwi·-g̣ádli

and you take that one's part

49

tsú·-da-x̯-kwi hú·mís-ditc, náu-nə-kwi·-g̣ádli

If you touch that poor thing (Cissy),

50

i-nantł-tcáʟ-kwə-ditc, wi-ɛ́n·ɛ-nantł-q̓álautaⁱ.

you will have to hurt me (first).

51

x̣-wɛ́n-íldᵘwa tłə-dɛx̣-ɛ́n·ɛ.

That is the way his mother spoke.

52

g̣ái-tłɛ-x̣-Cissy wi-p̓álst tłə-dətɛ́·tc,

Now Cissy tore up her (the Chetco woman's) clothes,

53

mánk̯t-łín·wi,

she beat her energetically,

54

wɛ́n-ditc-ix̯-hɛ́mɛ tła-ha-dú·ha'ya

(saying) ""So that's the awful kind of thing he has wanted

55

dlú·t'ci káⁱʻlis-dəmáix̣ᵘ

with her evil smelling and rotten vagina

56

wɛ́·n-ditc-ix̯ tła-ha-lín·wi dú·ha'ya dlú·t'ci káⁱʻlis

so that is the evil smelling and rotten thing he has wanted

57

x̣-wɛ́·n tłə-dɛx̣-gwə́lk̯dlɛ qdlá·t tlɛ-x̣-Cissy

That is the way her mother-in-law sided with Cissy.

58

á·yú-áɴ-q̓álutuwa tłə-dɛx̣-dɛ́·mił.

Then her husband did not injure her.

59

wí-dá-qlimniyu x̣ɛ́nx̣ɛnu tłə-dədí'lút'ł k̯̓ílga.

After that time her boy child became ill.

60

wí·-kwi yu-kwi-tsí·mq tłə-di̯k̓ílga x̣ɛ́nwəs,

While the child was lying ill,

61

wí·-kwí·-du tłə-di'yá·k̯da dɛ́n -g̯i-g̣aháis wí·-kwi sbái tłə-də'wá·l'wal.

her father-in-law every day ( everyday ) sharpened his knife.

62

kwi'yu-wí·-dɛ qʻt'sá tłə-dəmá·q ya-hantł-tłq̓áyaᵘ tłə-di̯-k̓ílga.

He was going to cut her throat if her ( kid of hers) child died

63

dɛ́n·g̯ɛk̯-g̣ahá·ya kwi·-sbá ⁱ tlə-dəwá·l'wal.

Every day he sharpened his knife.

64

wí·-x̣-wɛ́·n-du-di'lúwɛ tłi-Cissy,

Cissy thought thus,

65

h̓ú hɛ́i-x̯-hántł gwa-án du--q̓ú·s kwə-'nə'má·q̓

Hu! my throat is going to be rather tough (to cut)

66

kú-kwi dɛ́ng̣ɛ-g̣aháis kwi· sbái kwə-dəwá·l'wal.

with his sharpening his knife every day.

67

tsú-á·yu q̓áyaᵘ tłə-di̯k̓ílga

Her child did die then.

68

tsu-wɛ́·st-də́-k̓aʻ tłɛ-x̣-ɛg̣ɛ́·nu'wəs.

The people came back from the burial.

69

tsú-á·yu hwə́ldədzáhan tłə-dɛx̣-yák̯da niwá·l'wala

Now her in-law was going to leap at her with his knife.

70

tsú-hwə́ldi tłə-dɛdɛ́·mił.

But her husband jumped up.

71

áɴ-t'swá'al kwə́-dítc

Don't touch that poor thing

72

i-nantł-dú·ha'ya ma·q̓nə́-q̓x̣aⁱ,

if you want to cut a neck,

73

wí-ɛ́n·ɛ-'nə'má·q̓-nántł qʻt'sá·t.

cut my neck.

74

wɛ́·n-tłi-tłə-dədɛ́·mił íldᵘwa tłə-də'ɛ́łɛ.

That is how her husband spoke to his father.

75

tsú·-wɛ́·n-di'lúwɛ,

Then she thought,

76

h̓ə́ tcə'ɣín-da-x̯-wu-hɛ́mɛ,

Humph! he must think something of me,

77

náu-wuqdlədú·n.

to take my part.

78

x̣-wɛ́n-djinhɛ́·n tłə-Cissy x̣-wɛ́·n kwə-'n̥lag̣áwiyát̓a·s.

That is what Cissy thought ( that is what that she had thought)

79

wí·-kwi·-q̓áyaᵘ tłə-dədɛ́·mił,

Her husband died,

80

hís-tłə-dəmá·ni'yas tłə-dədɛ́·mił,

and also her husband's parents,

81

gu·s-íłk̓umts.

all of them died.

82

tłə-dəkwɛ́·nɛ́t'ł tła-ádzúʻ-dlɛ́·wɛ.

Only his two sisters remained alive.

83

wɛ́·n-tłi-Cissy wí-hit'cí·-tła-dlɛ́·wɛ tłə-dihí·mɛ.

Cissy then had only one child living.

84

wí-tłɛ-x̣-yuqú·nɛ ił'í·g̣ɛⁱ qʷcídjɛ-iłwə́s·i,

When they left Yaquina Bay to return south

85

wí·-gu·s-íł-g̣ɛ́ʻ tłə-tá·'laʻ-yug̣wutsɛ́·'mitc g̣ɛ́ʻ·ił gu-·s dlɛ́·qsiᴍ.

they all lived there at where the money digging (gold mining north of Bandon) was.

86

wí·-kwi n̥tslɛ́·nɛ́·-k̓aʻ kwi'íyɛ án-hú·dzi·t̓a.

Now the moving people began to bother with them

87

wí-kwi·-dá·s di'lút'ł-di̯k̓íłga,

Again she had a boy baby,

88

wí-wɛ́·n disáɴ tłə-bá·s-təni B.

by a white man named B.

89

kwi·-q'míya·t̓aⁱ-ditc dá' tłə-tá'la yúg̣wutsɛ́·'mitc.

He was cook there at the money digging (gold mine).

90

wí-wɛ́·n ił-du íldᵘwa hidjím·ił k̯̓ílgá·-tsa tlə-di̯k̓ílga wí·-di·łɛ́ⁱ wí·-cílɛ·t'citc-dlú·g̣wa,

They say this child was his own child, and today he (the son) lives at Siletz,

91

wi·-wɛ·'n-du-sínsá·nu C. B.

and he is named C. B.

92

wɛ́·n-disínsá·nu tłə-di̯k̓ílga

That is how they named her child,

93

tłə-dəbá·stəni̯-k̓ílga tłə-Cissy

who was the (moving person's) white's child by Cissy.

94

wí·-dá-qłəmniyu wí·-x̣-bá·stəni kwí·-dlug̣wá·qʷ tłə-Cissy wí-wɛ́·n-dísaɴ A.

After that a white named A. ( stayed with ) lived with Cissy,

95

wí·-dá·s hit'cí-di̯k̓ílga tłə-Cissy,

and Cissy had one more child,

96

wi-D.-disaɴ.

by the name of D.

97

wí·-his-hídji cilɛ́·t'c-itc dlú·g̣wa dí.

He also lives at Siletz at present.

98

wí·-tłə-Cissy wí·-q̓áyaᵘ tłə-dədɛ́·mił,

Cissy's man (A. ) died,

99

wí-his-hídji q̓áyaᵘ.

and then she died too.

100

dji·xwənt̓ɛ́-d(th)əkwɛ́·nɛ́t'ł.

She was the sister of djíxwɛ́nt̓î.

101

x̣-wɛ́·n gúmt̓u'ił-dəkúwi.

That is how all those (people) became gone.
