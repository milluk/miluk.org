A dream

28 lines · from Melville Jacobs’s Coos texts, dictated by Annie Miner Peterson (1933–34); keyed 1986–1990.

1

hí·mɛ-ł alicá·nida·ł wí·-xwí·dədí·ł.

We children were playing and rining.

2

hɛ́yu-má·tsi gwa·-dítc tsx̣á,

It was as if I stepped on something,

3

hɛ́u-má·tsi-wutú·ya'u.

and then I fell.

4

wí·-t̓ə́m·dzi·n íł-wu·tłə-x̣-hí·mɛ wí-wəsədí·n'íł'ú.

Those children carried me and took me ( to my house home ) home.

5

wí-hɛ́·niyɛ-wəx̣ɛ́nwəs,

I was sick for some time,

6

gú·s-mín-udzit'sí·ɣai,

I dreamed constantly,

7

an-wə́-dji·ʻ-g̣ɛ́·qł.

I could not sleep.

8

hɛ́i-má·tsi-k̓á'-dɛ́·djɛ hɛ́ndləs-dətɛ́·tc nəhɛ́·wudzən-dɛ́·məł.

(I dreamed) a person dressed in black, a fine looking man, entered.

9

hɛ́i-má·-tsi-x̣-kwí-t̓ím·dzú·n.

He carried me out.

10

hɛ́i-má·tsi̯-k̓ɛ́·lɛ-n̯ík̓in dá-tsí·m,

A good block of wood lay there,

11

wí-g̣ɛ́ʻ-wutsú·dzi·n.

he placed me on it there.

12

hɛ́i-má·tsi̯-ɛ́·k̓ tłə́tłt̓u tłə-dɛx̣-kwáʻkwí·nu,

(He took) a small axe from his pocket,

13

wí-x̣-kwi'yú·-kwi·-yáuq̓t tłə-'n̥djíl·ɛ.

with it he split open my leg.

14

wí-má·tsi-wɛ́n-wá·tsan.

That is what he did to it.

15

hɛ́i-má·tsi-gwa-gúwɛm-ditc-hál·gwíya.

He took out of it something like an angle worm.

16

w̯í-k̓iłánudɛ-nə́qt,

He held it in his hand,

17

dá-yuxúm·ɛ́ tłə-gúwɛm.

there the angle worm wriggled.

18

tsú-wɛ·n-tłí.

This is what he said then.

19

hámaq̓ni'íkwitc-ɛ́-ləmí·m-łə́n·i̯k̓

Do you see the grass standing there?

20

wɛ́n·antł-íl·dwa kwə-nɛ'ɛ́nɛ gwí·cdu·-nántł,

Tell your mother to cut (your leg) with glass,

21

tsú-hantł-t̓ə́m·a-kwí'yu·-sdłáq̓ai kwə-nədjíl·ɛ.

then she may wash your leg with that (grass).

22

hɛ́i-má·tsi-wɛ́·n-tłí'itc,

Then this is what he said,

23

mit'sə-sí·dainə'í

Do you know me?

24

k̓áʻ-dəmá·ni'yá·s-ú.

I am the father of the people (God)

25

tsí·-'nətsá·k̯índá·mi.

I came just to help you.

26

hɛ́i-má·tsi-máʻ-da-stú·q,

He just stood there,

27

wɛ́n-an-mátsi-an-ditc,

and then he vanished,

28

dá-daqłə́m·níyu tlə-wɛ́·n íl·at

after he had spoken thus.

The voice in these texts is Annie Miner Peterson’s, recorded by Melville Jacobs in 1933–34.
