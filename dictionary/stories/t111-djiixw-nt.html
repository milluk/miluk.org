Stories

Djiixwɛ́nt'î

61 lines · from Melville Jacobs’s Coos texts, dictated by Annie Miner Peterson (1933–34); keyed 1986–1990.

1

tłɛ-hɛ́·niyɛ tłə-n̥tsłɛ́·nɛ́·-k̓aʻ ał-áyu-kwi g̣ɛ́sdɛ'ɛyɛ,

Long ago when the moving people (the whites) first came here,

2

wí·-kwi- tłi-il-kwi sdáu-qʷts tłə-k̓áʻ tłɛ-x̣-n̥tsłɛ́·nɛ́·-k̓aʻ.

the moving people stealthily-attacked-before-dawn the (Lower Coquille) Indians (above Bandon Prairie).

3

wí·-kwi-íłk̓á·i nəhúm̯ɛ́-k̓ɛ'ɛ́'yu.

They killed women and all.

4

wí·-tli-íł-án-k̓á·i-də'ɛ́stis.

Others they did not kill.

5

wí·-tłə-tím·łi-hí·mɛ wí·-kwi·-tłə-xa·qáya-k̓aʻ wí·-tłi-iłk̓á·i-tłə́-k̓aʻ,

The boy babies the ( the moving honky people ) white people killed,

6

wí·-tłə-tím·łi-hí·mɛ wí·-kwi-iłk̓á·i.

the boy babies they killed.

7

wí·-kwi-tłə-djixwə́nt̓ɛ wí-t̓ə́ma̯-k̓ílga.

At that time djiixwɛ́nt'î was a child.

8

wí·-x̣-at̓á·di-yuxwúna.

His paternal aunt had him.

9

wí·-hí·m ɛ-húm̯ɛ́·k̓ɛ-dətɛ́·tc.

His garments were a girl-child's.

10

łú·-dí-tɛ·tc-wá·wa tłə-dɛx̣-át̓a,

That is the sort of clothes his aunt made for him,

11

áyú-ił-áɴ-tsá·u tłɛ-x̣-n̥tsłɛ́·nɛ́·-k̓aʻ.

so then the moving people did not kill him.

12

g̣ái-tlə-dəmá·-'niyas-g̯ɛ́· wí--kwí-ił-misá-ił̯k̓í·ya tłɛ-x̣-n̥tsłɛ́·nɛ́·-k̓aʻ.

But both his parents were killed by the ( whites ) moving people.

13

wí·-kwi· mán-gigwá-t's:nis tlə́-hitchit'cú·yu-də́-k̓aʻ.

He was already big when they drove the Indians (north).

14

x̣-wɛ́·n tłə-kwí-iłsə́dləní·m'ił-du tłə-də'át̓a.

Then he and his paternal aunt would hide.

15

x̣-wɛ́·n-ditc tłi-ił-hídji gwí·du.

That is how they remained alive.

16

wí·-dí'lút'ł tłi-hitchit'cú·yu-də́-k̓aʻ.

He was (still) a boy when they drove the people.

17

wí·-x̣-wɛ·n x̣-át̓ádə-kwi·-há·wiya.

So his paternal aunt raised him then.

18

wí·-wɛ·n-dísaɴ tlə-də'át̓a hu·'mi̯k̓ínɛn.

The name of his paternal aunt was hu·'mi̯k̓ínîn

19

wí·-x-wɛ·n tłə-kwi yá·x̣adja.

That is how they were at Yáhatc.

20

wí·-x̣-kuwí-ił-kwi-yuxwúna'aya,

Now some one (else) got him,

21

wí·-dá'-kwi·-hɛ́·-wi.

and he grew up there at that place.

22

wí-yə-hɛ́·wi wí·-kwi·-tłə-tcú·wáʻtcəs kwí·yɛ də-háihaya'ná·ya tłɛ-x̣- djí·xwə́nt̓ɛ.

When he had grown up djíxwɛ́nt̓î began to have an affair with Popeyes

23

wí-kwí-itctłx̯ínx̯iya wálaq̓as-dəg̣ála'yu·dja.

They caught the two of them within a clump of nettles.

24

g̣ɛ́ʻ-itctsí·m,

They were lying together there,

25

wi·-g̣ɛ-itcgíl·ya.

and they found them there.

26

wí·-kwi·-tli-íłtłantsá·u,

They were going to kill him,

27

tsi-tłə-dəx̣-at̓í·yada·s x̣-kwí·-kwi-iltsłə-tsúwit̓a.

but his two paternal aunts paid for his life.

28

wí-áyú ił-áɴ-dji-x̣áłi.

So then they did nothing to him.

29

an-íldú·há'ya hídji kwi·-də́hu·mis tłə-tcú·wáʻtcəs.

They (her family) did not want him to marry Popeyes

30

wí-g̣ɛ́sdɛ-dzínwɛⁿ x̣-hɛ́t'łɛ́·də.

Her brother brought her to here (to Coos Bay).

31

wí-má·tsi-n̥tsłɛ́·nɛ́·-k̓aʻ kwí·-yɛ-dihú·mis.

Then a moving person married her.

32

wí-tłə-djixwə́nt̓ɛ wí·-tłál·áu' ɛ kuwí-wamsts.

Then dji·xwɛ́nt̓î married Fat Face.

33

wɛ́n-má·tsi g̣ɛ́sdɛ́-iłmit'łda·'yá·s há·nisə́dja.

Now they were all living here at Empire.

34

wí-k̓úmts tłə-didítc tłə-tcú·wáʻtcəs.

The (maternal) relatives of Popeyes all died.

35

wí·-qʷcídjɛ g̣ɛ́ʻ-iłdlə́qʷsiᴍ tłi-iłdji·xwə́nt̓ɛ tłə-də'át̓a,

dji·xwɛ́nt̓î and his aunts went to the Lower Coquille to live,

36

wɛ́n-kwə-dəkwɛ́·nɛ́t'łəmɛ wí·-his-hídji dáʻ-dlú·g̣wa tłə-djixwə́nt̓ɛ.

and with his sisters djíxwɛ́nt̓î lived there too (near Prosper).

37

wí·-ta·-də́dɛ·mił tłɛ-x̣-tcú·wá'-tcəs,

Now Popeyes left her (white) husband,

38

adzú·-də'n̥tsłɛ́·nɛ́·-k̓a-hí·mɛ,

her two white person (half-breed) children (with her),

39

tsə-tsú-his-hídji qʷcídjɛ́-ła,

and she too went to the Lower Coquille,

40

náim-daʻ-dɛ' ɛ́łɛ tłə-tcú·wáʻtcəs,

because the father of Popeyes was there,

41

tłə-də'ɛ́łɛ dáʻ dibíndídza·s.

her father bíndídza·s.

42

wí-má·tsi kuwí-anya hu·t'súwa tłə-dəqɛ́·l-t'ci·'lɛ.

Now she was beginning to consort again with her former

43

wɛ́n-kwi·-há·g̣wiya tłə-dəhú·mis tłɛ-x̣-dji·xwə́nt̓ɛ.

Then dji·xwɛ́nt̓î left his wife (Fat Face).

44

má-itc-didjínu má·-kwi·-łáya-dədɛ́·mił.

It was her very own relatives who took her husband from her

45

hit'cí·-di̯k̓íłga tłə-dəhú·mis.

His wife had one child.

46

wí·-x̣g̣ɛt-í·g̣ɛⁱ ha·ní·cdja-wə́s·i,

She left there and returned to Empire

47

náim-g̣ɛ-dlɛ́·g̣ɛq-tłə-dəmá·ní'yas.

because her relatives lived there.

48

wí·-da-q̓áyaᵘ tłə-di̯k̓ílga.

Her baby died there

49

wí-ma·tsí·ya tcúwaʻtcəs-dəhú·mis.

Now Popeyes was his wife.

50

wí-his-tłə-dəhɛ́lu-hú·mis wí-his-kwí-q̓áyaⁿ.

Then his first wife (Fat Face) died too.

51

wí-tlə-dji·xwə́nt̓ɛ wí-máʻ-g̣úg̣wis lɛqlɛ́m.

So dji·-xwɛ́nt̓î continued to live on the Lower Coquille.

52

wí-dáʻ-g̣a-lə́lya-ítcdihí·mɛ tłi-itc-tcú·wáʻtcəs tłə-djixwə́nt̓ɛ.

There Popeyes and dji·xwɛ́nt̓î had ( a lot of ) many children.

53

wí-his-hídji dá'-q̓áyaⁿ tłə-djixwə́nt̓ɛ,

Then dji·xwɛ́nt̓î also died there (about 1924),

54

wí-his-tłə-dəhú·mis-q̓áyaⁿ.

and then his wife likewise died

55

án-ha-hɛ́·niyɛ,

It is not very long ago,

56

gwa-daʻ-t̓í·c-dji-idzí·mis,

perhaps about ten years,

57

tłi-itc-misá-q̓áyaⁿ.

that they both died.

58

wí·-gú·s-yɛ́-iłq̓áyaⁿ,

Now they are all dead,

59

hit'cí-hú·mis wɛ́n-hit'ci-dɛ́·mił.

(excepting) one woman and one man.

60

kuwí-tła gwí·dɛt tłə-djixwənt̓ɛ́-dihí·mɛ.

They are the only ones living of djíxwɛ́nt̓î's children.

61

wɛ́·s-wu-tła-k̓wá·ni-yada.

That is all I know of that.

The voice in these texts is Annie Miner Peterson’s, recorded by Melville Jacobs in 1933–34.
