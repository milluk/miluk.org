Stories

A woman obtained fir power and learned two medicines

31 lines · from Melville Jacobs’s Coos texts, dictated by Annie Miner Peterson (1933–34); keyed 1986–1990.

1

tsú-wɛ·n-dəg̣wə́ns tłə-hú·mis·

A woman dreamt in the following manner.

2

tsú-łá,

She was going along,

3

nik̓·ínu·-wút'łi,

she stepped over a log,

4

hɛⁱ-ná-tsi gwúldəsəm tłə-ník̓·in.

and then the log rolled over.

5

ə̓ⁿ gu·s-mín ɛ́n·ɛ-'nəg̣wá·niyu dzɛ́·x̣ɛx̣ɛⁱ

(Said the log, ) Oh I they're always straddling over me.

6

k̓·i-náu-'nə'lúwɛ

My heart is (I am) getting tired of it!

7

gu·s-mín ɛ́n·ɛ-'nəg̣wá·niyu dzɛ́·x̣ɛx̣ɛⁱ

they're always having to straddle over me

8

hɛⁱ-má·tsi án-wɛn-əx̣wálx̣wal,

Now his (fir log's) eyes were bad,

9

łqwə́l-tłə-dəx̣wálx̣wal.

his eyes were red. (He appeared as a person in her dream.)

10

ɛ́dji̓

Come (fir tree said to her.)

11

alicá·nú·s-hantł.

Let us play a game.

12

wɛ́n-snantł alicá·níwa·s.

This is the way we'll play.

13

miyúhán á·yu-itc-maʻ-wɛ́·n.

This is the way they played poke-punch game.

14

wí·-má·tsi-ha-kúwi dzág̣wi tłə-dəgá·t's.

He merely poked (with a finger) at her breast (nipples).

15

tsú-dlánk̓·ts.

She awakened.

16

hɛⁱ-má·tsi-á·yu-x̣ɛ́nwəs tłə-dəgá·t's,

Her breasts were indeed sore,

17

wɛ́n-tłə-də-x̣wálx̣wal dədjɛ́·nɛ́'nis,

her eyelids too,

18

wí·-łqwl.

just as red (as fir's eyes).

19

hɛⁱ-má·tsi-dá·s kwi·-də́g̣wəns.

Then she dreamed again.

20

kwí·-nántł-yúqʷda kwítc-kwi·-łə́n·ik̓·,

You are to pick and gather (M.) e<n:ik! weeds,

21

kwi'yú-nantł-łá·łi kwə-nəgá·t's.

you will doctor your breasts with them.

22

wɛ́n-kwə-nəx̣wálx̣wal,

And as for your eyes,

23

wí·-kwtc-ɛ́·'lə́'ma kwi'yú-nantł-łá·łi kwə-nəx̣wálx̣wal.

you are to doctor your eyes with that other one yonder.

24

hɛ́ ⁱ-má·tsi-dá·s-nik̓·í'níyɛ.

Then again he became a log.

25

tsú-dlánk̓·ts.

She awakened.

26

hɛ́ ⁱ-má·tsi-áyu-his x̣ɛ́nwəs-tłə-dəgá·t's,

Indeed now her breasts were also sore,

27

wɛ́n-his-tłə-dəx̣wa lxwal.

and her eyes too.

28

tsú-a·yu-kwi-kán·i tłə-dəłɛ́·ł.

So she went and tried those medicines indeed.

29

hɛⁱ-á·yu-kwi-łhɛ́u tłə-dəgá·t's,

sure enough they healed her breasts,

30

wɛ́n-his-tłə-dəxwálx̣wal.

and also her eyes.

31

kwi'yu-łá·łi tlətc-wɛ́·n-íldwa tłə-dəłɛ́·ł.

She cured them with what he told her were their medicines.

← Hadjíyasa, tłɛ́iɣəl, and others Getting rid of a thunder dream-power →

The voice in these texts is Annie Miner Peterson’s, recorded by Melville Jacobs in 1933–34.
