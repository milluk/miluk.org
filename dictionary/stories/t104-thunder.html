Stories

Thunder

36 lines · from Melville Jacobs’s Coos texts, dictated by Annie Miner Peterson (1933–34); keyed 1986–1990.

1

tłə-lag̣áwiyára·s,

According to the tale,

2

i-du-t'sə́n·a-íl·at wí-wɛn-ił-du-íldⁿwa.

when thunder spoke they said this.

3

bɛ́lɛx̣ɛ·nu-tsá·-du náim-ilhú·dat tłə-dihí·mɛ.

He is angry because they are humiliating his children.

4

wí-wɛn-du-íldⁿwa tłɛ-x̣-k̓áʻ tłə-t'sə́n·a

And this is how the people would speak to thunder.

5

bíldjɛ-ła'yáq̓aⁱ.

Go on north!

6

g̣ɛ́ʻ-hú·dɛt tli-nihí·mɛ.

they are insulting your children there yonder.

7

tsú·-il-du-t̓ə́ma g̣ɛ́ʻ-x̣wáiłu idjáu-tłi-iłdidáhaⁱ,

Then they would leap there to where they had their tobacco,

8

wí-hɛ́milt̓idjɛ ił-du-xⁿk̓wáⁱ.

and they would throw it into the fire.

9

wí-his-t'łɛ́hɛ,

And also a paddle,

10

wí-his̯-ɛ́·k̓-dət'łais,

and likewise a little part of a net,

11

máʻ-ditc kwi-íł-du-hɛ́milt̓idjɛ xⁿk̓wáit.

various things they would throw into the fire.

12

sk̓idá·mí·łin.

We are compensating you.

13

í·g̣ɛⁱ

Go away!

14

bíl-djɛ-ła'yáq̓aⁱ

go on north!

15

á·yú-du-łá-dəgwə́lɛ·t.

Roaring he would go indeed.

16

x̣-wɛ́·n-ił-du-wá·wa tłə-t'sə́n·a

That is how they did that to thunder.

17

án-huwɛ tsi-tłə-k̓áʻ-kwí k̓a'ái-k̯łá·wi,

That was the reason why if a ka saw him as if he were a ka

18

wí-wɛ́·n-íldⁿwa tłɛ-x̣-t'sə́n·a

this is the way thunder would speak to him.

19

yu-wántł du-bɛ́lɛx̣ɛ·nu,

When I become angry,

20

wí·-łu-tcíl-hantł-du-ním dáhaⁱ,

you are to give that tobacco to me,

21

wɛ́·n málqs.

and also paint.

22

kwí·-tła wá·g̣a-ditc ɛ́ni.

Those are the things I value big (highly).

23

wɛ́n íldⁿwa tłɛ-k̓áʻ k̯̓x̯íya'yɛis.

That is the way he would speak to the ka to whom he talked

24

x̣-wɛ́·n tlɛ-x-k̓áʻ kwi-tłq̓á·ya.

That is why that person would follow (believe) that.

25

wɛ́n-ditc-du tli-ił-kwí hɛ́milt̓ídjɛ łú-xⁿk̓wait.

That is why they threw those things into the fire.

26

án-huwɛ tłi-ił-t'sə́n·a án-íłhu·dat,

The reason they did not shame thunder,

27

náim-his-kwí-x̣·wɛ-n djíʻ-tłi-łnɛ-'ɛ́n·ɛ má·ni'ya·s,

(was) because he was just like our own father to us,

28

his·kwí-x̣-wɛ-n tłə-t'sə́n·a.

that is the way thunder was too.

29

hís-kwí-x̣-wɛn-łú·didá·ya tłɛ-x-t'sə́n·a,

Thunder also took care of them in the same manner,

30

x̣-djí tłi-ł-ú·didímidu·n tłi·łnəx̣-ɛ́n·ɛ-má·ni'ya·s.

just as our father watched over us.

31

wí-i-na-áɴ-tłq̓á·ya tłə-t'sə́n·a,

If you did not believe in thunder (saying insultingly),

32

h̓ə́ má·tsi t'sə́n·a

Ha! it is only thunder!

33

má·-da-wántł kwi·-álqsa

why should I fear it?

34

hɛⁱ-dú-á·yú'itc hidjú·wi łú-tú·ya tłə-lúwa·qʷ,

then sure enough lightning would fall on such a person,

35

wɛ́n-x̣-kwi·-tsaⁿ.

and it would kill him.

36

x̣-wɛ́·n-ditc tłə-x̣-k̓áʻ kwi· łín·wi tłq̓á·ya.

That is why the people followed (believed) that strongly.

The voice in these texts is Annie Miner Peterson’s, recorded by Melville Jacobs in 1933–34.
