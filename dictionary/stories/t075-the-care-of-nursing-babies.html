The care of nursing babies

71 lines · from Melville Jacobs’s Coos texts, dictated by Annie Miner Peterson (1933–34); keyed 1986–1990.

1

tłi-hí·mɛ tsú-hit'cí idzɛ́·mis-du dá-dkəhɛ́\niyɛ́ɛis bí·łədjɛ́·-du,

Until children were about one they still put them ( the children) in cradle,

2

sɛx̣símx̣-ɛ́q̓hɛm.

they tied them in it.

3

tsú-du hit'cíyɛ-di'idzí·mis tsú-du-ánya-sɛx̣símx̣ɛ́q̓hɛm.

When they had become a year ( old ) they did not tie them in so much

4

tsú-du-t̓ə́ma g̣ɛ́ʻyuxwúmɛdí·wɛ tłə-hí·mɛ.

Then children began to go (crawling) around.

5

x̣-wɛ́·n-du-tłi-iłdihí·mɛ.

That is the way children were.

6

mái-du-tłə-hɛ́·wi ma-íł-du-dzəsdláq̓.

No matter how big they were their mother still bathed them.

7

i-du-sdlɛ́·q̓ɛ łáʻ-tłə-hú·mis,

When the woman went for a bath (in a creek),

8

wí-t̓á·mí-du tłə-di̯-k̓ílga.

she packed her baby along.

9

wí·-tɛ́·tcítcdə du-dləg̣wá·qʷ.

She sat it on her clothes (as she bathed).

10

yí-ɛ́·wi-dəsdlɛ́·q̓ wí-t'łhátsəm

When she finished bathing she dressed,

11

wí-hɛlt̓-sdláq̓ tłə-di̯k̓ílga.

and then she gave a bath to the baby.

12

tsú-t̓ə́ma-t̓á·mi wɛ́n-wəs·í'itc.

Then she packed it and that was how they went back home.

13

tsú-t̓ə́ma dlug̣wíya i-itcwɛ́·st.

She set it down when they returned.

14

tsú-t̓ə́ma g̣aqmíyat̓í·wa,

She began cooking,

15

x̣-wɛ́·n-du wí-q̓átsk tłə-di̯k̓ílg

she was going to feed the baby.

16

í-ɛ́·wi dədlu'wá·was wí-yu-g̣witq̓wí·du,

When it finished eating it became sleepy,

17

wí-tsú·'ya,

she put it to bed (in its flat cradle),

18

wí-idjɛ́·-dəhɛ́·niyɛ'ɛis wɛ́n-dəhɛ́·niyɛ'ɛs-g̣ɛ́·qł.

and it could sleep any length of time.

19

wɛ́·n-du tłi-hí·mɛ.

That is the way babies (were cared for).

20

i-dú-n̥tca'á·yaq̓,

When it began to walk,

21

wí· tsú-t̓ə́ma s'yái̯k̓it.

now they (the parents) had sexual intercourse.

22

tłə-di̯k̓ílga łú·-du tłi-ił'ál-qsa tłɛ́-x̣-k̓áʻ,

The people feared for their children until then,

23

ná·yím-du x̣ɛ́nx̣ɛnu tł̯í-k̓ílga.

because the children always became ill

24

náu-ił-kwí·-łín·wi-álqsa tłɛ́-x̣-k̓á.

The people were extremely afraid of that.

25

náu-ił-du-án-t'swá·l tłi-iłduhú·mis,

(Until then) they never bothered their wives (sexually),

26

i-du- ɛ́·k̯̓-íldi̯k̓ílga,

if they had a small (nursing) baby,

27

wí-an-ił-du djí·-x̣áłi tłí-iłduhúm̯ɛ́·k̓ɛ,

they did not do anything to their wives,

28

náim-łínwi-ił'álqsa.

because they were extremely fearful.

29

yuwút's q̓áyaⁿ tłi̯-k̓ílga,

Maybe the child might die,

30

í-s'yɛ́i̯k̓u.

when there was sexual intercourse.

31

x̣-wɛ́·n-ił-du-íl·at.

That is what they said.

32

wí-íl·ax̣q̓ain ił-du·-g̣áłám.

They got a shaman.

33

ú̓ áɴ-x-k̓xáⁱ

Oh! it is no pain-power

34

tsí-s'yɛi̯k̯ɛ́·k̓

there has merely been sexual intercourse

35

wɛ́n-du-íl·at tłə-íl·áx̣q̓ain.

That is what the shaman said.

36

wí-á·yú-du łú·nú·-du łá·łi łú·-diłɛ́·łitc tłɛ-x̣-íl·áx̣q̓ain.

Then the shaman doctored with the (healing) medicine (herb) for that.

37

áɴ-huwɛ tłi-ił·ínwi-kwi-álqsa,

They were so very much afraid of that,

38

náim-i-di'lút'ł-di̯-k̓ílga wí-yi-múwɛ'líyɛ,

because if she had a boy baby while she was pregnant,

39

wi-yuwút's gwɛ̯'ɛ́k̓-di̯k̓ílga'áida,

and maybe there was a girl baby (in her womb),

40

i-di'lút'ł-di̯k̓ílga,

while she had a boy baby,

41

wí·-łín·wi-du-x̣ɛ́nwəs tłə-dí'lút'ł̯-k̓íłga.

then terribly ill the boy baby (would be).

42

wí-i-gwɛ́'ɛ̯k̓ tłə̯-k̓ílga,

And if (she had) a girl baby,

43

wí·-yántł mú·l-tit̓úwa tłə-dəx̣-ɛ́n·ɛ,

and the mother would become pregnant,

44

wí·-his-kwí-x̣-wɛ́n,

and it would be that same way too (a boy in her womb),

45

łín·wi-du-x̣ɛ́nwəs.

terribly ill (the girl baby would be).

46

wí-í-his-kwí-gwɛ́'ɛ̯k̓ tłətc-mú·haltit̓úwa,

But if it were also a girl with which she was pregnant,

47

wí-aɴ-ha-x̣ɛ́nwəs.

then it would not be so ill.

48

his-kwí-x̣-wɛ́n tłə-dí'lút'ł,

And similarly with a boy,

49

i-di'lút'ł da-qłə́mníya'wa,

if the next one were (also) a boy,

50

wí-aɴ-łín·wi-x̣ɛ́nwəs.

he would not become so ill.

51

an-dú-húwɛ tłi-ił-kwí·-k̓wá·'niya·da tli̯-k̓ílga tłə-kwi·-x̣ɛ́nwəs,

The reason they knew what was the sickness of a (childhood) child,

52

i-du-s'yɛ́i̯k̯ɛ́·k̓,

when it was (due to parental) sexual intercourse,

53

wi-aɴ-dú·há'ya dlú'wíyam.

(was that) it did not want to eat.

54

má·tsi t'łɛ́·lɛl gú·s-mín,

Its eyes were constantly shut,

55

wɛ́n-má·-tsi gwa-ámi-lá·mak̯,

and it was just as if without bones,

56

má·tsi-kálsisá·nu.

just limp.

57

x̣-wɛ́·n-ditc tłɛ-x̣-k̓á tłə-kwí·-k̓wá·-'niyá·da x̣-djí tłi̯-k̓ílga x̣ɛ́nwəs,

That is how the people ( around) knew what the child ( little childs ) was sick from,

58

náim-du ɛ́nɛdi-gát'si,

because it was nursing its mother,

59

x̣-wɛ́·n ł'áitəm tłə-də-x̣ɛ́nwɛ́'ɛs,

and that is how the sickness started,

60

tlə-dɛx̣-ɛ́nɛ-dəgá·t'sətc.

from the mother's breast.

61

wɛ́n-ditc i-iłk̓wá·'ni'ya·da'áya tłi-ił-múwɛl,

That is how they knew when they were pregnant,

62

wi-anya-iłgát'si th́-iłdi̯k̓i'lga,

then they no longer nursed their child,

63

náim-x̣g̣ɛ́t tłə-kwi·-x̣ɛ́nx̣ɛnu.

because it was from that they became ill.

64

wɛ́·n-ditc tłí-ił áɴ-dji-x̣áłi tłí-iłdəhú·mis,

That is why they did nothing with their wives,

65

yúwút's g̣ɛ́nt'cínsi idzí·mis,

sometimes for five years,

66

yúwút's psínł idzí·mis.

maybe for three years.

67

tsú-ił-t̓ə́ma wá·tsan tłí-iłdəhú·mis.

Then they began to do it to their wives.

68

wí-áɴ-x̣ɛnx̣ɛnu tłi̯-k̓ílga,

Then the children did not become ill,

69

yu-wɛ́·n-ditímɛ·ł.

if they were of that age.

70

an-úmit'ssíyada-ditc tłə-s'yɛ́his dəłɛ́·ł,

I don't know what their medicine was for pregnancy sickness,

71

ditc-lə́n·-i̯k̓ tłi-iłdəłɛ́·ł.

(or) what kind of weed medicine.
