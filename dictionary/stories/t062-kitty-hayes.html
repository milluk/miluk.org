Stories

Kitty Hayes

46 lines · from Melville Jacobs’s Coos texts, dictated by Annie Miner Peterson (1933–34); keyed 1986–1990.

1

tłə-tłɛ́iɣəl-digwá·ya wí·-x̣-ládjəs kwi·-tłá·wi.

Rogers was the one who bought the daughter of tl=ɛ́i*ł1

2

adzú-ni̯k̓ín-kwi·-x̣-wɛ́·n-dag̣wə́láqʷ.

Two sticks (ð̥2,̥̥) he paid for her.

3

wí·-tłə-də'ɛ́nɛ wí·-há·g̣wiya tłə-tłɛ́iɣəl.

Her mother left tl=ɛ́i*ł1.

4

qʷcídjɛ́-wə́s·i.

She came back south (i.e., to Coos Bay).

5

wɛ́n-má·tsi t̓ími·duwɛ kwi-an-dá·s-kwi-dəhú·mis tłə-tłɛ́iɣəl.

From then on tl=ɛ́i*ł1 no longer had her ( his wife ) for his wife.

6

má·'aya-dəhúm̯ɛ́·-k̓ɛ·

He had other wives.

7

wí·-x̣-t̓ími·dúwɛ tłə-Kitty-Hayəs k̯̓ɛ́·lɛ-hú·mis-áł'áyu.

At the start then ( she was ) Kitty Hayes was a ( good ) good wife.

8

wí-x̣-ɛ́nɛ́də kwi-álháit.suwa.

But her mother had spoiled her (injured her character).

9

maq̓á·la náim maq̓ált-kłə-də'ɛ́nɛ.

She was jealous for her mother had been jealous.

10

wí-x̣-t̓ímidúwa áɴ-Wɛn.

So from that time on she was no good.

11

wɛ́n-má·tsiya-hɛ́ihɛyɛ'nu tłə-dədɛ́·mił.

Then her husband began to consort sexually

12

wí-idjáu-du̯-k̓íldwa tłə-dədɛ́·mił,

Where-ever she found her husband,

13

wí-kwi-dú-ga·s-tsáu tłə-hú·mis.

she would nearly kill that woman.

14

wí-má-x̣-wɛ·n gú·s-mín-wá'nu.

He did it all the time.

15

wí-hit'c̯í-k̓íłdwa-tłə-hú·mis dáʻ-gum-tłə-dədɛ́·mił,

Once she found a woman when her husband was there,

16

wí-kwi-gúm-t'cá·l-tłə-hú·mis x̣-t'sám,

and she burned the woman with a burning brand,

17

x̣-kwi'yú-gum-t'łnix̯dá·-dɛg̣ɛ́ʟ tłə-hú·mis.

she thrust it in the woman's crotch.

18

x̣-wɛ́·n maq̓ál·is.

That is how she was jealous.

19

wí·-kwi-tłɛ-x-yá·hatc í·g̣ɛⁱ-də́k̓aʻ

The people left Yáhatc,

20

qʷcídja-wá·sdidíyam.

they returned to the south (to Coos Bay).

21

hít'cí-dəgwá·ya tłi-ił-xg̣ɛ́t-í·g̣ɛⁱ tłɛ-x-yá·hatc.

When they left Yáhatc she had one daughter.

22

wí·-g̣ɛsdɛ́ʻ-wá·sdidíyam.

They came back to right here (to Empire).

23

wí·-ga·s-tsáu-tłə-dɛx̯ -dɛ́·mił,

Her husband nearly killed her,

24

náim-sgí·nam tłə-kwi-wutwúndi·t̓a tłə-dədɛ́·mił,

because he found out she had lied to her husband.

25

wi-yu-kwí-sg̯ú·-nam wí-kwi'yɛ́·nu-ga·s-tsáu.

When he found out she'd lied he nearly killed her for that.

26

tsi̯-x̣-k̓ílgadə-gɛ tłə-kwi-nəqa·'ya,

Had not her child saved her,

27

náu-t̓ə-ma-ax̣tłə-tsáu tłə-dɛx̣-dɛ́·mił,

her husband would have killed her that time,

28

náim-hɛ́wɛ·su·tc g̣wəsg̣wái.

because she had told a lie.

29

má·-hidjím·ił nɛ́·-djis,

It was only her own trick (fault),

30

tłə-kwí·-da dlándisa·t̓a tłə-dəmí·disa·ma dú·dɛ,

to shut in his own nephew,

31

tłə-Jack-ládjis-dədú·dɛ.

Jack Roger's nephew.

32

łín·wí-kwi·-x̣án'wúnam tłə-kwi-hɛ́wɛ·s·-g̣wə́sg̣wai tłə-dədɛ́·mił.

He took it so hard (got so angry) because she had told such a lie to her husband.

33

wí-x̣-wɛ́n-'nɛ́t tłə-kwi-gá·s-tsáu.

That is why he nearly killed her.

34

wí-x̣ɛ́nx̣ɛnu tłə-dədɛ́·mił,

Then her husband became ill,

35

wí-an-hɛ́·niyɛ-dlɛ́·wɛ.

and he did not live long.

36

tsq̓á-yaᵘ.

He died.

37

wí-wɛ·n-ił'íldᵘwa.

This is what they said.

38

gwá·-x̯-tsa·-g̯ɛ x̣-hídji tłɛ-x̣-Kitty-Hayəs tłə-kwi-g̣á-yuq̓ayaᵘ níya-tłə-dədɛ́·mił

They claimed that Kitty Hayes herself had given poison to her husband.

39

x̣-wɛ́·n-ditc kwi·-q̓áyaᵘ tłə-dədɛ́·mił.

That is the way her husband died.

40

wɛn-kwi·-má·tsi-k̓úmts tłi-dihí·mɛ,

Then all her children died,

41

wí-tłi-hídji wí-má·tsi-áɴ-wɛ́n hú·misíyɛ.

and she became a bad (loose, drunken) woman.

42

má·tsi-gu·s-míɴ-lá·'ma

She lay drunken all the time.

43

wɛ́n-má·tsi-x̣ɛ́nx̣ɛnu.

Then she became ill.

44

amí·sg̯i gu·s-mín-án-hu·-t'su·tim.

She did everything for nothing (i.e., for drink).

45

má·tsi-gú·s-míɴ łín·wi-lá-'ma.

She was just completely drunken all the time.

46

wɛ́n-ma·tsi-his-hídji kwí·'nɛ'wɛt'ł q̓áyaᵘ.

Then she died poor. (She was about forty at her death).

The voice in these texts is Annie Miner Peterson’s, recorded by Melville Jacobs in 1933–34.
