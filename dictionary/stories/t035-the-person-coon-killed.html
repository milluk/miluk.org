Stories

The person coon killed

25 lines · from Melville Jacobs’s Coos texts, dictated by Annie Miner Peterson (1933–34); keyed 1986–1990.

1

kwə-k̓áʻ t'sx̣wə́n·aɣ k̓úmwá·wa

The persons coon killed

2

tsú-kwí-hú·wɛ'ɛ́tsəm'ɛ́m·ɛ tłə-tcá·nə́ɣa t'sɛhɛ́iyɛ́·'nu,

Young men were making preparations to go trapping,

3

wɛ́·n-his-hántł łɛ́mdí·da

and they were also going to hunt.

4

wí·-kwi·-hi·t'cí-iłtsá·u tłə-t'sx̣wə́n·ɛɣ,

They killed one coon,

5

gú·s-dítc-iłtsá·u.

they killed various other things.

6

wí·-tsú·-kwi-g̣atq̓á·idiya,

Now it was becoming evening,

7

tsú-iłt'cíl-ɛ́.

and so they made a fire.

8

ú· t'sx̣wə́n·ɛɣ łhántł-q'máit̓ts.

uu/ let us roast the coon beside the fire.

9

tsú-ił-á·yu-kwi·-iłskán-i.

Then indeed they put it on the spit (roasting stake).

10

wí·-i-kwí·-g̣atłq̓álx̣au wí·-g̣ɛ́ʻt'sándədí-wa,

When it became hot it began to shrivel,

11

wí·-kwi·-gu·s-djí·yɛ-t'sándu.

and then it was all drawn and shrunk.

12

wí·-kwi·-íłha'lá·wau tłɛ-x̣-tcán·ɣa

Now the young men laughed at it (it looked so comical).

13

hit'cí·-tła-án-g̣ɛ́idlɛ.

Only one of them did not laugh.

14

wí·-tłɛtc-t'łɛ́ux̣ɛx̣ɛi wí·-án-dji-kwi-ɛ́·wi-kitskí·yɛx̯,

Those who did laugh (laughed so hard) could not quit laughing,

15

wí·-má·tsi-ił-k̓úmts

and so they died.

16

tskí·x̯ix̯i-kwi·-k̓úmts.

The laughing killed them

17

tsú-wə́s·i-tłə-dí·luł,

Now the (serious) young man returned,

18

k̓wɛ́·n-bíya

he took back the news.

19

wɛ́·n-íl·-at-i-wɛ́·st.

This is what he said when he came home.

20

k̓úmts-tłə-'nɛ'ɛ́stis.

My comrades died.

21

tsú·-áyú-ła--də́-k̓aʻ-g̣ɛ́ʻ,

Now then the people went to there,

22

tsú-iłwá·s-diya-ɛ́q,

and they brought back the dead,

23

tsú-ił' ɛ́g̣ɛ́·nitɛ́m.

and they buried them.

24

má·tsi-x̣-wɛ́n-k̓um-tłə́-tcá·nəɣa,

And it is that way that the young men died,

25

t'sx̣wə́n·aɣ-kwi·-k̓úmwá·wa.

it was (laughing at) coon that killed them.

The voice in these texts is Annie Miner Peterson’s, recorded by Melville Jacobs in 1933–34.
