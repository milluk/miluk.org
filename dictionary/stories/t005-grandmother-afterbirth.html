Grandmother afterbirth

27 lines · from Melville Jacobs’s Coos texts, dictated by Annie Miner Peterson (1933–34); keyed 1986–1990.

1

wí·-tłhwíyɛ-də-úmnát'łətc,

When the grandmother (afterbirth) was born,

2

x̣-kwí-tsá·-du aq'álqsit̓úwa tłə-k̓·ílga.

that is what scared the baby.

3

wɛ́·n-ditc-du tłə-du-kwi-su'wáini dɛhɛ́ʟ tłɛ-x̣-q̓á·ná-k̓·ílga.

That is why the very young infant drew (puckered, grimaced) its face.

4

wɛ́n-ił-du-íldᵘwa tłɛ-x̣-k̓áʻ.

That is what the people said.

5

tłɛ-x̣-hu'míkca tłə-kwí-aq'álqsit̓úwa,

It is the old woman who scared her,

6

wɛ́·n-ditc-tłə-kwi-su-'wí·naⁱ.

and that is why it grimaced.

7

x̣-wɛ́·n-ditc-tłɛ́-x̣-k̓áʻ tłi-ił-du-kwí·-g̣ɛʻ-mɛg̣ɛ́nt,

That is why the people danced there,

8

hí·mɛ́·-du g̣ɛ́ʻmagání·da.

the children danced there.

9

g̣ɛnt'cínsi g̣aháis tłə-hu'mík̓·-x·iná'adja iłmɛg̣ɛ́nt,

They danced five days for sake old woman laid up above there,

10

náim-ił-kwí·-tłq̓ya.

because they believed that.

11

yúwút's-du-áx̣ats i-du-kwí·-aq'álqsit̓úwa tłɛ-x̣-hí·mɛ dihú'mik̓.

Sometimes it cried when the old ( old ) woman scared the baby.

12

kwí-iłtłq̓áya tłɛ-x̣-k̓áʻ,

The people believed that.

13

wɛ́·n-ditc-du tłi-ił-kwí·-q̓atská·n tłə-hú·'mik̓· gɛnt'cín-si g̣aháis,

That is why they fed the old woman ( old woman ) for five days,

14

náim-i-ił-áɴ-x̣-wɛ·n,

because if they did not do that,

15

wí-aq'alqsit̓úwa tłɛ-x̣-hú·'mik̓·,

the old woman would scare it,

16

i-ił-á ɴ-x̣-wɛ·n-wá·tsa.

if they did not treat her thus.

17

wɛ́n-ditc-du tłi-ił-kwí-mag̣ánidiya g̣ɛnt'cínsi g̣aháis.

That is why they danced five days.

18

t̓ə́ma-tsá·-du há·g̣wiya tłi-k̓·ílga tłə-dɛ-x̣-hú'mik̓.

Then the old woman left the child.

19

i-du-hwutłhwíyu tłi-k̓·ílga intqántłtsí·t̓á·-du,

If when the baby was born there was a black bruise on it,

20

wí-wɛ·n-du-íldⁿwa tłɛ-x̣-k̓áʻ,

the people said as follows,

21

x̣-úmná·t'łətc tłə-kwí·-tg̣ántłts.

It is grandmother who bruised it.

22

ła'yáq̓ai̓

Go on

23

tłɛ́ silt̓í·yi̓x̣

get outside quickly

24

wɛ́·n-tsá·-du-íldⁿwa.

That is what she said to it.

25

wɛ́n-ditc-du tłə-kwí·-notqantłtsí·t̓a.

That is why it was bruised black.

26

dag̣ána·tł-tsá·-du i-áɴ-tłɛʻ-sílt̓.

She punched it when it did not go outside quickly.

27

wɛ́·n-du-iłlág̣awiyat̓ana'ya tłɛ́-x̣-káʻ tłə-kwí·-n̥tqantłtsí·t̓a tłi-k̓·ílga·

That is what the people explained about babies that were bruised (black) black.

← How a child was frightened, and later taught to be fearless Hadjíyasa, tłɛ́iɣəl, and others →

The voice in these texts is Annie Miner Peterson’s, recorded by Melville Jacobs in 1933–34.
