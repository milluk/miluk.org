A deserted poor woman was given food by shags

62 lines · from Melville Jacobs’s Coos texts, dictated by Annie Miner Peterson (1933–34); keyed 1986–1990.

1

tłɛ-hɛ́·niyɛ wí-kwi·-tłə-hú·mis gu·s-míɴ-du·-kwídlag̣áunaⁱ·

A certain woman long ago was soliciting handouts all the time.

2

wí-kwi-gínwut'súwa lúwɛ tłɛ-x̣-k̓áʻ,

The people's hearts got tired of it,

3

ná·im gu-s-míɴ kwi-hítchidjínaⁱ.

because she was coming everywhere all the time.

4

wi·-g̣ɛ· lu wiyɛ tsú-tsłíntsim-də/k̓aʻ.

When winter was approaching ka were going to move away.

5

tsú-wɛ·n-ɣa·lánu də́k̓aʻ,

The people spoke thus,

6

há·g̣wiya·ł-hantł.

We will leave her.

7

hɛ́lt̓-ha gú·s-míɴ kwí·-k̓a-t'ci·łiłínaⁱ.

She is always bothering the people too much.

8

wí·-wɛ·n-ɣa·lá·nu.

And then this is what they said.

9

há·-g̣wiya·ł-hantł ił-hantł-tsłíntsiᴍ.

We will leave her when we move away.

10

wɛ́·n-iłɣa·lá·nu tłɛ́-k̓áʻ

That is what the people said.

11

á·yu tsłíntsim-dðə́k̓aʻ,

Indeed the people moved away,

12

á·yu-há·g̣wiyu,

they left her,

13

mi·t'cí-kwi-dá'-há·g̣u.

they left her alone there.

14

gɛdjímin-tsłíntsim tłə́-k̓aʻ·

The people moved across to the other side (of the water).

15

dɛ́ngɛ-g̣aháis ma-íłłú·dədaya ya-hantł-g̣wə́l·i tłə-di'yɛ́·ts.

But every day they watched to see if there would be smoke from her house.

16

hɛⁱ-dú-á·yu-itc g̣wə́l·i·-du,

And indeed there was smoke from it,

17

i-ił-du·-łú·dədaya dɛ/ngɛ-q̓ɛłí·'mis.

when they looked for it each morning.

18

ma-íłłú·dəda·ya tłə-dəg̣wə́-l·ɛ́' ɛs.

They continued to watch her smoke.

19

gu·s-míɴ-du·-qánu·-dlú·g̣wa dɛ́ngɛ-g̣ɛ́ʻłim,

Every morning she was always seated outside,

20

ma·-dú-qanu-dlú·g̣wa tłə-hú·mis.

the woman still sat outside.

21

ʟɛaᵉ mɛ somɛthing (Dump somɛthing out of thɛ boat for mɛ̓)

ha<lkvwdi-mtciL

22

x̣-wɛ́n-du-íldⁿwa tłə-tłɛ́stłɛs i-du-kwí·-ł'áʻ-yim.

That is what she said to a shag when going (by) in the air.

23

halkʷdí·mtciʟ

Dump out something for me

24

kwə-tcilnɛdí·bu-t'łu.

Fill up your dip nets for me.

25

kwi·-tcílhalkʷdi·m

Give me that much

26

míɴt'ci g̣ɛ́ʻłim,

One morning,

27

hɛⁱ-má·tsi k̓áʻ djíyan'áma'itc,

and then people (shags) were coming up the river,

28

g̣ɛnt'cínsi tłgú·s tłítc-k̓áʻ dzín·wi.

five canoes of those people were coming up.

29

gú·s-kwi/ hɛ́ⁱ-má·tsi iłhí·q̓at,

Then they all landed,

30

wɛ́n-g̣ɛ-ił-á·yu hít'ci-dí·bu'wi,

they each took one dip net there,

31

g̣ɛ́ʻ-dlág·i tłə-báldisidja·

they poured it out there on the beach.

32

tsu-iłgisgínt'łu.

Then they went on.

33

tsú-g̣ɛʻ-tɛ́ixɛᵘ,

She went down to the water,

34

q̓wə́n·yau-dðəg̣a·/ł g̣ɛ́'-dzí·dzɛ,

she labored over all sorts of food there,

35

hít'cí-g̣aháis kwi·-dzí·ya tłə-dəq̓wə́n-yau.

all day long she worked on those foods.

36

dɛ́ngɛ-g̣ahá·ya mɛ́-x̣-wɛ·n.

It was like that every day.

37

g̣ɛ́ʻ-kwi·-t'łú·-du-tłə-də'yɛ́·ts.

Her house there was filling up (with foods).

38

x̣-wɛ́·n g̣ałə́łya-dəq̓wə́n·yaⁿ,

That was the way she obtained quantities of foods,

39

ga·-síya t'łú·du tłə-də'-yɛ́·ts.

nearly filling up her house.

40

x̣-wɛ́n á·yu x̣-tłɛ́stłɛs kwí-q̓wə́nyau-niya.

That is how shag gave her foods.

41

x̣-k̓á'ái kwí·-k·łá·wi,

She saw them as if (they were) persons,

42

mín·t'ci háma·q̓áma,

but the last time she saw them,

43

hɛ́ ⁱ-má·tsi áɴ-k̓á'itc,

(they were) not persons at all,

44

hɛ́ ⁱ-má·tsi tłɛ́stłɛ́s'itc·

(they were) just shags.

45

tsú-wɛn-ɣa·lá·ni·da.

Then this is what (the people) said.

46

háma-wántł g̣ɛ́ʻ-sədz ɛ́·wɛ.

I'll go over to see how she is.

47

má·-ł-ami q̓wə́nyau kwi·-há·g̣wiya,

We left her quite without foods,

48

máʻ-kwi-áiwa dá-x-dlɛ́·wɛ,

but she seems apparently alive yet,

49

áiwa-du g̣wə́l·i tłə-də'yɛ́·ts.

there is still smoke coming from her house.

50

áyú-hit'cí-k̓aʻ g̣ɛ́ʻ g̣ál·ats.

So one fellow crossed over to there.

51

má·tsi-gwa-t'cú·lá"ya i-dɛ́·djɛ.

He was astounded when he went inside.

52

t'łúʻ-tłə-də'-yɛ́·ts x̣-q̓wə́nyaᵘ.

Her house was filled with foods.

53

dáʻ-gwa-djilt'sái tłə-k̓áʻ-g̣ɛ́ʻ-dji,

The person who came there was somewhat abashed,

54

náim-ił-kwi-há·g̣wiya ámi-q̓wə́n·yaᵘ

because they had left her without food.

55

wɛ́·n-ditc tłɛ́-x̣-k̓áʻ tłi-ił-kwí·-a/ɴ hú·dat tłə-kwí'nɛ'wɛt'ł-k̓á'

That is why the (wealthier) people never made fun of poor people,

56

náyím-du yuwút's-kwi x̣-dítc-du-kwí·-k̓wí·ná'ya.

because it was possible that something 'd take pity on them,

57

wɛ́n-du-ɣa·lá'nu tłə́-k̓áʻ.

That is what the people said.

58

wɛ́n-ditc tłi-ił-áɴ-kwí'nɛ'wɛt'ł-k̓aʻ án-hú·da·t,

That is why they did not ridicule poor people,

59

wɛ́·ntc tłi-ił-kwí-an-hú·dat tłə-kwí'nɛ'wɛt̓'ł-k̓aʻ.

that is why they did not make fun of poor people.

60

hɛ́ ⁱ-ił-du·-kwi ha-k̓·ílɛ-łú·dədá·ya,

They looked after them even better (than others),

61

mái-kwí·'nɛ'wɛt'ł-hí·-mɛ hís-ił-kwí·-íł-k̓·ílɛ-łú·dəda·ya,

even poor children ( poor children ) they looked after better too,

62

náim-du-x̣-g̣aháis-kwi·-k̓wí·ná'ya·

because the day (a power) would take pity on them.
