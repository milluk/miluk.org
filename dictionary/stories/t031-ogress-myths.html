Ogress myths

43 lines · from Melville Jacobs’s Coos texts, dictated by Annie Miner Peterson (1933–34); keyed 1986–1990.

1

nú·sgíli bá·saq̓

Ogress myth

2

gú·s-k̓áʻ-g̣ɛ́·-ła-t'sɛhɛ́m-di'yáqt'sa

All the people (many people) went there to cut up a whale.

3

wí·-g̣á··ł-tłə-hí·mɛ,

There were a great many children,

4

wí·-gú·s-mín-kwi-mag̣ání·da

and they were dancing all the time (often)

5

hɛ́i-má·tsi-ditc-x̯lə́m·a·

Now then something (bad) entered (was about to enter).

6

wí·-wá·'nu-tłətc-hú·'mi̯k̓,

The old woman (who stayed at home) said,

7

gú·s-tciʟsdlə́ntsəm

All of you hide

8

tsú·-x̯lə́m·a tłə-nú·sgíli hu·'m̯ɛ́·k̓ɛ,

Now then two ogress women entered (and said),

9

kwíyał-hántł-mɛg̣ɛ́nt

Let us dance

10

tsú·-t̓á·mi tłə-dətgídzən,

She (the old woman) carried her granddaughter on her back,

11

tłətc̯-ɛ́·k̓-tgídzəndə

her tiny granddaughter (covered over and hidden there).

12

tsú-ił-g̣ɛ́mə-g̣á'niya,

Now then they danced,

13

tsú-wɛ́n-mɛg̣ɛ́nt nú·sgíli,

and as the ogresses danced (one of them asked),

14

wí·-wan-dítc-nət̓á·m nəx̣-hí·t'ci

What are you packing, my half (my sister)?

15

kwí·-nð-ditc-t̓á·m,

what are you packing,

16

nəx̣-hí·t'ci

my half?

17

tsí-'nðts'ái

It is only my back.

18

tsí-u-wɛ́·n-hɛ·'wə́q̓hɛm

I just grew that way.

19

wí·-hɛi-dú·ha-kwi·-dzə́g̣wi tłɛ-x̣-nú·sgíli

Now the ogress wanted to touch it.

20

ámi-k̓á·ʻ-dəg̣aháis'i

Is there absolutely nobody (at home)?

21

g̣ɛ́ndji g̯ɛ́·kwi·-ła'áyam kwi-hí·mɛ,

Where did those children go,

22

gu·s-mín-kwi mag̣án·í·da tłə-hí·mɛ

those children who were always dancing?

23

wí·-mit'ssíya·da tłɛx̣tc-hú·'mi̯k̓ s'á·t'ł-dú·-də'wɛ́·t'ł·

That old woman had recognized them by their pitch dresses.

24

tsu-g̣ɛ́ x̣-t'sám t·c·á tłə-də'wɛ́·t'łə́djɛ,

So then she put a lighted torch to their dresses,

25

áyu-djict'cíł·u,

indeed they caught on fire,

26

tsu-x̣wá·iłu tłə-nú·sgíli

and then the ogresses dashed outside.

27

tsú·-ct'cíʟ tłi-itc-də'wɛ́·t'ł,

(When) their dresses burned,

28

tsú-də-ma-x̣wá·iłu.

then they jumped outside.

29

áyu-wɛ́n-ła'áyam,

Indeed they went like that,

30

gú·s-dítc-cú·t'łu i-dáʻ-ła'áyam-də-hwíyɛ́·t.

everything caught on fire as they went running along.

31

i-dú·-ct'cíl-wí·-kwí-du də́m·a·-du-i-tú·ya,

When they burned they fell down with a sound "boom,

32

wí-məsá-itcct'cíl tłə-nú·sgíli.

both of the ogresses burned up.

33

wí-má·tsi-gú·s-kwí-k̓úmts-tłə-hí·mɛ,

However, all those (hidden) children had already died,

34

hís-tłitc-t̓ám tłə-dəx̣-úmná·t'łətc,

even that one packed by his grandmother,

35

hís-kwí-q̓áyau.

he died too (because the ogress touched him).

36

g̣ɛ́ʻ-ła'áyam tłə-k̓áʻ ła'á-yám·ə́djə,

She went to where the people had gone,

37

g̣ɛ́ʻ-ła tłətc-hú·'mi̯k̓,

the old woman went to there,

38

yə-ánya-k̓wɛ·nɛ́·nu wí-hís-hídji-má·tsi-q̓á-yau.

and when she had finished recounting it she herself died too.

39

yə-wɛ́·st-də-k̓áʻ,

When the people got back home,

40

wí·-ámi-hí·mɛ,

the children were gone,

41

gú·s-kwí-q̓áyau-tłə-hí·mɛ

those children were all dead.

42

wɛ́n-ditc-dú·-tła-gátq̓ai án-hí·mɛ álica·ní-da,

That is why children must never play at night,

43

łú--x̣ú·t'łuc-g̣ɛ́ʻ-íksəm.

lest such dangerous beings get amongst them.

The voice in these texts is Annie Miner Peterson’s, recorded by Melville Jacobs in 1933–34.
