He starved his mother's sister

62 lines · from Melville Jacobs’s Coos texts, dictated by Annie Miner Peterson (1933–34); keyed 1986–1990.

1

t'łáɣ·i'ya dəxunk̓wə́n

He starved his mother's sister

2

xᵘk̓wə́ndə́-kwi·-há·wau tłi̯-k̓íł·ga.

The child was brought up by his mother's sister.

3

wí-kwi·-hɛ́·wi.

Now he had grown up.

4

gu·s-mín-du·-ɛ́hɛ dləám·łɛ,

He was away spearing fish all the time,

5

ámi-du·-q̓wə́n·yau-wɛ́·st.

but he would come back without food.

6

wɛ́n-kwi-du·-łg̣ɛ́·n tłə-dəxunk̓wə́n.

That is how his aunt became hungry.

7

mín·-t'ci·,

Once,

8

ɛ́ʻdjiʻ

Come here

9

hámanan-x̣wut'łdá·mi.

let me hunt on your head (for lice).

10

á·yu-x̣wát'łi tłə-dəxunk̓wə́n.

So then his aunt hunted on his head.

11

hɛi-má·tsi gɛ́·qł-itc

Then he fell asleep.

12

hɛi-má·tsi nəq̓wə́n'ya'wa tłə-dəg̣ɛ́t'sg̣ɛⁱ.

Now his teeth were full of food.

13

tsú-anya x̣wát'łi,

So she quit hunting on his head,

14

tsú-dłan̯k̓ts.

and he awakened.

15

ɛ́·-gwɛ́·gwi

Oh aunt

16

dlə́m·łɛ́-wantł.

I am going to go spear fish.

17

á·yu-ł·á

So he went indeed.

18

tsú-x̣-sədłə́ntc,

Now she hid,

19

úmi-dúwa tłɛ-x̣-gwɛ́is.

the girl followed him (unseen).

20

tsú-x̣-sədłə́ntc łú·dədáya.

And she hid and watched him.

21

hɛi-á·yu nɛg̣ɛ́łyɛq dzág̣wí-itc

My but the salmon he is spearing

22

hɛi-má·tsi yɛ́·dzədjɛdə́-ł·a,

Then he went housewards,

23

wɛ́·n-t'cíłɛʻ.

and he made a fire.

24

sədłə́ntc łú·dədaya tłə-dɛ-xunk̓wə́n.

His aunt watched him as she kept hidden.

25

tsú-g̣ɛ́ʻ-x̯í·ła x̣-sədłə́ntc.

Hidden she looked in to there then.

26

hɛi-má·tsi t'łú tłə-də'yɛ́·ts x̣-q̓wə́n·yau,

Now his house was full of food,

27

wɛ́n-kwi-du·-łg̣ɛ́·n tłə-dəxunk̓wə́n.

and his aunt was hungry.

28

hə̓ kwí·nɛ́wɛt'łi u-kwi·-háwiya,

Humph! I had a sad task raising him,

29

wɛ́·n-x̣-kwi wu-áɴ-ditc ni·'ní·wun,

and now he does not give me a thing,

30

łg̣ɛ́·n'wu-du,

and I am hungry,

31

wí·-tłi-hídji wí·-g̣á·ł-dəq̓wə́nyau.

while he himself has quantities of food.

32

tsú-wi-wɛ́s·i tłə-gwɛ́is.

Then the girl went back home.

33

tsú-t̓á·mi-dəhadái'mis,

Now she packed her valuables,

34

tsú-í·g̣ɛi,

and then she went away from there,

35

tsú-má·-t'łdáicdja djíʻ.

and she came to another place.

36

idjáu łəqłɛ́·m kwə-hɛthɛ́·dɛ

Where does the wealthy headman live

37

ɛ́·g̣ɛ łɛqłɛ́·m.

He lives there yonder.

38

á·yu-gɛ́ʻ-łá tłə-gwɛ́is,

The girl indeed went to there,

39

tsú-ditdjíyu.

and they took her in.

40

hɛthɛ́·dɛ kwíyɛ-dəhú·mis.

She became the wife of the wealthy head man.

41

tsú-wɛ́·st tłɛ-cdjí-t̓əs dí·łúł.

Young man kingfisher got back home now.

42

áɴ-ditc tłə-dəxunk̓wə́n,

His aunt was nowhere,

43

tsú-úmiduwa tłə-dəxunk̓wə́n,

so he followed his aunt,

44

hag̣adí·dðu-úmidədí·t̓a.

he followed her tracks.

45

á·yú-ł·a,

Indeed he went along,

46

tsú-g̣ɛ́ʻ-djiʻ.

and then he got to there.

47

idjáu-tłə-'nəxunk̓wə́n

Where is my aunt

48

ɛ́·g̣ɛ-nantł-ła,

Go yonder to there,

49

kwɛ-hɛthɛ́·dɛ-dłú·g̣wa'adja.

to where the wealthy head man lives.

50

a·yú-g̣ɛʻ-ła,

Indeed to there he went,

51

tsú-ditdjíyu,

and they took him in,

52

tsú-q̓atskí·nu,

and they gave him food,

53

tsú-g̣ɛ́ʻ-dłu'wí·wɛ.

and there he ate.

54

áɴ-k̓wí·ni-q̓atskí·nu.

No little food they gave him.

55

áɴ-hɛ́··niyɛ dlú'wíyam,

He was not eating a long time,

56

hɛi-má·tsi ditc-tłá·uwa'itc

and then something burst.

57

ditcə́x̣·a'ya ɛtłá·uwa

What exploded

58

hm· ɛn·ɛ́·-da

Hm it must be me.

59

x̣-wɛ́n-tli-tłə-dí·łúł.

That is what the young man said.

60

á·yu-hídji tłə-tłá·uwa,

Indeed it was himself who burst (from overeating),

61

tsú-q̓áyau.

and then he died.

62

x̣-wɛ́·n tłə-cdjít̓əs łag̣awiyá·t̓as.

That is the way it was with the kingfisher in the narrative.
