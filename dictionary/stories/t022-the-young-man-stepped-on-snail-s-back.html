The young man stepped on snail's back

68 lines · from Melville Jacobs’s Coos texts, dictated by Annie Miner Peterson (1933–34); keyed 1986–1990.

1

tłə-dí·lú·ł bú·lák̯-dəts'ái tsxá·ts

The young man stepped on snails back

2

dí·lúł gú·s-mín-du-kwí-yuxúm·ɛ́.

A young fellow was always going about here and there.

3

k̓úmts-də́'má·ni'yas-ditc.

His parents were dead.

4

x̣-hɛ́-t'łɛ́·də-kwi·-há·wau.

It was his older brother who raised him.

5

mínt'ci-hwuthwí·d,

One day he was running about,

6

hɛi-má·tsi-bú·lak̯-tsxá,

and then he stepped on a snail,

7

wí·-ku-idlí·-t'łu tłə-bú·lák̯.

and it broke the snail (shell).

8

wɛ́n-kwi̯-ɛ́·k̓ qłdidjá·di-lá·lu,

That was the way a tiny piece of it got stuck in his foot,

9

wi-án-dji-kwi-hálkʷt.

and he was unable to get it out.

10

wɛ́n-má·tsi̯-x̣ák̓i'íya-tłə-dəqłá,

Then he just gnawed at his foot,

11

wí-i-kwi̯·-x̣ák̓i wí-wudə́n-yáicdjadə-lá·lu·

and while gnawing at it the blood got in his mouth.

12

ú··-hɛi-gwə-án-t'ccə́l·əs ku-wúdən

Oh how sweet tasting that blood is

13

wɛ́n-má·tsi-wə́s·i.

he just went back home.

14

wɛ́n-má·tsi-dlə'ú·nis-dɛ'ɛ́stis.

Now then he began to eat up his people.

15

gú·s-kwí--łdjá-tłə-k̓áʻ-mit'łdá·'yá·s.

He ate up all the people in his village.

16

wí-hit'cí-dəgwə́l·a.

He had one younger sister.

17

hɛ́i tsú-kwi·-djí-tłə-dəgwə́l·a,

Now his younger sister arrived,

18

má·tsi-án-wi̯-k̓il·-dwa.

and she saw no one (there).

19

hɛi-má·tsi-kwi·-dlú·g̣wa-tłə-də'mitłgwə́la.

Only her younger brother was living there.

20

ɛ́·k̯̓-di̯k̓íl·ga-tłə-gwɛ́is.

The girl had a small baby.

21

dzə́n-'u-kwə̯-k̓íl·ga

Give the baby to me

22

án-g̯ɛ́·-mɛ́ʻ

Not just yet younger brother

23

t'sálbən-du tłə-di̯k̓íl·ga,

She deliberately pinched her baby,

24

á·yu-du-áx̣·ats.

and indeed it cried.

25

á·iwa-tsə-nantł níya'mi.

I will give it to you after a while.

26

háma-wantł-g̯ít'sda,

I will nurse it first,

27

tsú-nantł-t̓ə́ma-níya'mi.

then I will give it to you.

28

dá·s-du-t'sə́l·ban-tłə-di̯k̓íl·ga,

She pinched her baby again,

29

á·yu-du-áx̣·ats,

and indeed it cried.

30

ɛ́lqsədú·n·ɛ.

He is so afraid of you,

31

háma-wantł-sdlá·q̓t.

I will bathe him first.

32

tsú-nantł-t̓ə́ma-níya·'mi.

Then I will give him to you.

33

g̣átq̓ai tłə-gwi-sdlá·q̓t.

She bathed it in the evening.

34

tsú-ma·tsi-t̓á·-mi-tłə-di̯k̓íl·ga,

Then she packed her baby,

35

tsú-n ɛ́qɛ-tłə-gwɛ́is.

and now the girl fled.

36

wə́s·i-tłə-dədá·míłdja.

She went back to her husband.

37

tsú-k'ál·a,

Then she hallooed,

38

tłɛ·-tcílłá·dzədai

Come over quickly and get me

39

x̣-dítc-u'úmidədú·n

there is something pursuing me

40

á·yu psə́nł̯-k̓áʻ g̣al·a'ts tłɛ'ɛ́i-də-ił-g̣ɛ́ʻ-dji.

three went across by canoe so they'd get quickly to her

41

á·yu-má·tsi dándanu-tłə-tłgwə́ls isgáʻ-hwə́ldi tłgú·'sidjə tłə-gwɛ́is.

just as the canoe neared the beach the girl leaped immediately into the boat.

42

tłɛ́·-tcilła-x̣ú·t'łuc u'úmidədú·n.

Go quickly.

43

tsú-á·yu.

A dangerous being is pursuing me.

44

i-hi·-q̓ádəma.

Then indeed (they made haste) so.

45

tsú-sg̣ú·ya-tłə-dədɛ́·məł.

When they beached she told her husband.

46

x̣ú·t'łuc tłə-'nə'mítłgwə́la.

My younger brother is a dangerous being.

47

tsá·utcil-hantł.

You will (have to) kill him.

48

gú·s-k̓áʻ-łdjá.

He ate up all the people.

49

tsú-á·yu·

Then (they did) indeed (as she ordered).

50

mán-his-hídji k'ál·a.

He was already hallooing too (from the other side).

51

tsú-iłqʷłái hɛ́məlt̓djɛ ił'wánqt.

Now they piled rocks onto the fire.

52

tsú-ił-t̓ə́ma qáʟ-hu·t'súwa,

And they dug a hole,

53

tsú-ił-q'wá'as.

and they got planks.

54

tsú g̣atłq̓álx̣au tłə-qʷłái,

Now the rocks were getting hot,

55

tsú-ił-g̣ɛ́' łt'łáut tłə-qá·li'idjɛ,

so they put them down into the hole there,

56

tsú-ił-q'wá'as gɛ́ʻ-hidíya.

and they lay the boards over it,

57

tsú-g̣ál·ats-tłə-k̓áʻ-kwi·-łá·dza.

Now they went across to fetch that (dangerous) person.

58

hú·-h ɛ́l

Ho brother-in-law

59

k̯̓ílɛ́·-nantł-dlú'wiyam.

You will eat well.

60

á·yu-hi-t̓íu,

Indeed they brought him back home,

61

á·yú-q̓átskí-nu,

indeed they gave him food.

62

tsú·-gwa'ás-dlú'wíyam tsú·-djit'cdjíu tłə-q'wá'as

Then in the middle of his eating they pulled off the lumber,

63

á·yu-g̣ɛ́ʻtú·ya tłə-qál·dja

and so indeed he tumbled into the hole.

64

tsú-t̓ə́m·a g̣wá·niyu q'wa'ás g̣ɛ́ʻwáng̣ayu,

Now they piled up there on top of the lumber,

65

tsú-iłg̣ɛʻxiní·m t̓łə-k̓áʻ.

now the people were on top of it there.

66

á·yú-gɛ́'t cil

Indeed he burned up (down in) there.

67

wɛ́·n-iłtsá·u.

That is how they killed him.

68

wɛ́·n-ditc tłɛ-x̣-k̓áʻ kwi·-ál·qsa tłə-bú·lak̯-dəbáʟ tłɛ-x̣-k̓áʻ·

That is why the people ( the people fear those ) fear snail shells.
