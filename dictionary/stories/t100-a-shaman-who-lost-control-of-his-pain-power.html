A shaman who lost control of his pain-power

9 lines · from Melville Jacobs’s Coos texts, dictated by Annie Miner Peterson (1933–34); keyed 1986–1990.

1

tłə-k̓á íl·áx̣q̓ain ya-ányá-tci náqt tłə-dək̓xáⁱ,

If a shaman could not hold his pain-power,

2

wí-má· tə́mɛ́·t'łɛ íl·áx̣-q̓ain-du g̣álám.

he got another old shaman.

3

wí-á·yu-du-x̣-kwí· tsá·kán i-du-ánya-djí-kwi-naqt tłə-dək̓xáⁱ.

He as the one who assisted hirn when he could not hold his pain-power.

4

ná·im yuwút's mí·dəsá·madə hí·mɛ k̯̓í·ya.

Because he might kill his own children.

5

wɛ́n-ditc-du tłə-də-kwí· dú·ha'ya ax̯íya tłə-dək̓xáⁱ.

That is why he wanted to cool the pain-power.

6

náim álqsa yúwut's hí·mɛdə kwí'yu̯-k̓í·ya.

Because he was fearful lest he kill his own children with it.

7

wɛ́n-ya-áɴ-djí dá·s-łú·dədáya.

That is how he was unable to take care of it any more.

8

wí-his-hídji q̓áyau x̣-kwí'yu.

He might even kill himself with it too.

9

wɛ́n-ditc-du tłə-kwi-x̣-má· íl·áx̣q̓ain tsá·kan

That is why the other shaman helped him.

The voice in these texts is Annie Miner Peterson’s, recorded by Melville Jacobs in 1933–34.
