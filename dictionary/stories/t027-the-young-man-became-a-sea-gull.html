The young man became a sea gull

42 lines · from Melville Jacobs’s Coos texts, dictated by Annie Miner Peterson (1933–34); keyed 1986–1990.

1

tłə-dí·lúł wá'asíya

The young man became a sea gull

2

mí·t'ci kwí--ləqlɛ́·m tłə-hú·'mi̯k̓,

The old woman was living alone,

3

wí·-kwi·-há·wau-dədím·sin.

she was bringing up her grandson.

4

an-tłá-du. g̣ɛ́wi yuxʷum·ɛ́-n

Never travel uphill in yonder direction

5

áɴ-wɛ́·n-g̣ɛ́ʻ,

It was bad there (she said),

6

á-yu-du-án-g̣ɛ́ʻ-yuxúm-ɛ́.

and so indeed he never went there.

7

gu·s-mín-du-wɛ́·n-íl·dwa tłə-dəx̣-úm·ná·t'łətc.

His grandmother was constantly telling him that.

8

hɛ́··wi tłə̯-k̓íl·ga,

The child grew up,

9

dí·luł·íyɛ.

he became a young man.

10

tsú-g̣ɛ́·wi kwi-bá·məs-daɣdáɣ.

Now (he thought he would go) up above to eat salalberries.

11

mɛ́ʻ-g̣ɛ́·wi-ła.

So he did go up anyway.

12

hɛi-má·tsi hu·m̯ɛ́·k̓ɛ,

And there were women (there),

13

bá·məs-iłyúg̣wa.

they were picking salalberries.

14

ú·· k̯̓íl·mu

Oh nephew

15

k̯̓íl·ɛ́-x̯-də-n-dí·-dji

it is fine that you have come here.

16

gu·s-mín-ł-du dú·ha'yał-du-k̯łá·wihən.

We have always been wanting to see you.

17

k̯łú·da·míhən.

I saw you all the time.

18

tsú-wə́s·i-tłə-dí·lúł

Now then the young man went back home.

19

x̣gɛ́n'wə́snə

Where have you been?

20

wɛ́·n-íl·dᵘwa-tłə-dəx̣-úm·ná·t'łətc

That is what his grandmother said to him.

21

má·-u-áɴ-x̣gɛ́n'wəsə.

Why I have not been anywhere.

22

hɛ́wəs·dú'nə

You are lying.

23

wɛ́-n-ildá·mi,

And I told you so,

24

an·ántł-du-g̣ɛ́ʻ-yúx̯úmɛ,

never to go (up) there,

25

wí-g̣ɛ́ʻ-nax̯łá.

and then you went there anyway.

26

tsúʻ-mank̯t tłə-dədím·sin.

Now she thrashed her grandson.

27

tsú-x̣g̣ánau tłə-dí·lúł

The young man wept.

28

g̣álam-tłə-dək̓ú'uts

He got his play canoe.

29

t'łɛ́wɛ́-səs

Wind

30

łə́n-wi-q̓ɛ́wɛ·si'í·yɛ

blow hard

31

á-yu g̣ɛsq̓ɛ́u-su

Sure enough the wind blew.

32

ɛ́··· tłə-dəyɛ́·ts kwí·-p̓icicíyam tłətc-hú·'mi̯k̓,

ee/ee the old woman's house blew away,

33

tsú-his-hidjí-p̓c·íᵘ tłətc-hú·'mi̯k̓

and then the old woman herself blew away slso.

34

wí·-wɛ́·n-tłi-tłə-dí·lúł,

And then the young man said (sang) thus,

35

hə'ə́· hə́'ə́· g̯ɛ́-'nə'úm·á·ci

He'ee/ he'ee/ My grandmother

36

kwí·yuk-p̓cíᵘ tłə-'nə'úm·na·t'łətc

The wind blew away my grandmother.

37

kwí·'nɛ'wɛt'ł i-u-kwí·-hɛ́·wudzu·n,

She had so hard a time bringing me up,

38

wɛ́n-kwi-úp̓cáʻ'uwá·wa

And then I made her blow away.

39

an-wántł-dá·s-díu-dlú·g̣wa

I will not remain here longer.

40

bál·diya-wántł yux̯úmɛ.

I will go about on the ocean beach.

41

á·yu ánya-q̓ɛwɛ́·-si,

Indeed the wind blew no more,

42

á·yu-bál·diya ayá'yux̯úmɛ tłə-dí·lúł.

and to be sure the young man went about on the ocean beach.
